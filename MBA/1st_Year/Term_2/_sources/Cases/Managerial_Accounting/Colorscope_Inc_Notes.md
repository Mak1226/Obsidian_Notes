# Colorscope, Inc. — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Colorscope_Inc.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBS case. Spring 1996. **Andrew Cha**, founder of **Colorscope, Inc.** — a small southern-California pre-press / color-separation shop for advertisers and catalogers, founded 1976, sales peaked at $5M in 1988 — turned down a ~$10M R.R. Donnelley acquisition in 1988, then watched cheap PC/Mac desktop-publishing and local service bureaus commoditize pre-press (page price fell from ~$1,100 to ~$600, 1991–95). In May 1994 the client that was **80% of his business** took pre-press in-house. Rebuilding, Cha has started collecting June 1996 job-level data — revenue by customer (Exhibit 8), hours by workstation by job (Exhibit 9), **rework hours split by cause** (Exhibit 10), financials (Exhibit 11) and materials by job (Exhibit 12) — and must decide how to improve operations, how to reprice, and what accounting/control system a firm that "cannot afford expensive accounting systems or ABC consultants" should install.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **The process (activities to cost):** an order is taken and "prepped" by a customer-service rep who owns it → **Scanning** (3 laser scanners digitize images) → **Assembly** (9 high-end networked Macs; operators compose the page, match colors/shades to spec) → **Output** (four-color film) → **Quality Control** proofing (compare to customer spec; **rework is initiated here**). QC can send a job back for **rescanning** if the scan was flawed → reassemble, re-output, re-proof. Operators are cross-trained; procedures are **standardized but not documented** — the firm runs on institutional knowledge and Cha's frequent supervision.
- **Pricing today:** roughly the **same per-page price for every customer**, plus a fee for special effects — even though customers place very different demands on scanning, assembly, proof rounds and rework.
- **Rework tracking:** line workers are hourly and log job hours from remote terminals; Colorscope now records rework hours **separately by cause** — (a) **customer-initiated** (change in specs after submission) and (b) **in-house error**.
- **Industry:** US pre-press ~$6B (1995), fragmented, overcapacity → continuing price decline. Competitors: integrated printers bundling pre-press + printing (Donnelley, Quad), national trade shops with sales networks (American Color, Wace), and standalone shops next to big accounts. **Colorscope lacks a sales infrastructure.**
- **Cha's questions:** How to improve operations? How to change pricing? What accounting and control system to install?

### Central decision
- Using the June 1996 job-level data, **determine which customers and jobs Colorscope actually makes and loses money on** once activity costs and rework are properly assigned; then decide **how to reprice (or renegotiate/drop) unprofitable customers**, **how to reduce rework and who should bear its cost**, and **what right-sized activity-based costing / control system** to adopt while rebuilding after the loss of the anchor client.

### Key takeaways
- **Build activity cost pools:** Job Preparation, Scanning, Assembly, Output, Quality Control, plus materials. Direct labor is hourly and logged by job; equipment/overhead is largely fixed and should be spread on a **driver per activity** (machine- or labor-hours), not a flat per-page rate.
- **ABC vs. the flat rate:** costing the June jobs by activity shows that simple, few-image, few-change jobs **subsidize** image-heavy, high-change, high-rework jobs; some average-priced customers are deeply unprofitable and others very profitable.
- **Rework is the profit killer — and cause matters:** **customer-caused rework** (post-submission spec changes) is a service Colorscope gives away — charge for it or cap changes in the quote. **In-house-error rework** is a **cost of quality** to drive toward zero (documented procedures, better first-pass scanning, QC upstream), and its cost should be charged back to the department that caused it (often Scanning) to create accountability.
- **Customer profitability → action:** rank June customers by revenue minus fully-loaded activity cost including their rework; keep and grow the profitable ones; for the rest, reprice to true resource use, renegotiate scope, or exit — Colorscope no longer has the volume to carry loss-making accounts.
- **Pricing strategy:** replace flat per-page pricing with a **menu tied to cost drivers** — per image/scan, assembly complexity, per proof round, and per customer-requested change — kept simple enough to quote by phone.
- **Control system:** Colorscope already collects the raw data it needs (workstation hours by job, rework hours by cause, materials by job); a lightweight job-costing rollup to **job- and customer-level profitability each month**, plus **documenting the undocumented procedures** (which also reduces reliance on Cha's personal supervision — the very asset Donnelley valued), is the right-sized answer.
- **Strategy:** pre-press is commoditized; Colorscope's edge is a fast, skilled team and special-effects capability, not price. Costing discipline + rework reduction + differentiated pricing for complex work + gain-sharing with employees on efficiency gains is the path back — not chasing volume at market prices it can't profitably serve.
