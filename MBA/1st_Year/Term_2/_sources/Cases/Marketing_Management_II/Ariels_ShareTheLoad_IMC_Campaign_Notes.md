# Ariel's #ShareTheLoad: Integrated Marketing Communication Campaign — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Ariels_ShareTheLoad_IMC_Campaign.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Thunderbird case (Batra). **Pankaj Srivastav** (pseudonym), marketing head for **P&G's premium detergent brand Ariel** in India, is reviewing the performance of **#ShareTheLoad** — a purpose-led IMC campaign launched in 2015 around gender equality in household chores ("doing laundry is not a woman's job"). Six years and four "chapters" later (Dads → Sons → women's well-being → "equal sleep"), it is a genuine cultural phenomenon: WARC named it the world's most effective campaign, it pushed the share of men who think chores are "a woman's job" from **79% (2015) to 52% (2019)**, and it lifted Ariel from #44 to **#7 among India's Most Trusted Brands**. Yet Ariel's **premium-segment market share has barely moved** (~1.4% vs. Surf Excel's ~20%), and Surf Excel's emotionally powerful "Daag Achhe Hain" is a strong counter. Pankaj worries about campaign wear-out and whether a renewed campaign will finally translate into share.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Market:** Indian laundry care ~INR 302.2 billion, +4% (2020). **HUL dominates ~43% of retail value; P&G ~9%.** Premium segment = Ariel vs. Surf Excel; **Ariel brand share only ~1.4%** (Exhibit 3: Surf 20.1%, Ghari 18.1%, Wheel 14.1%, Rin 8.2%, Tide 7.6%). P&G has struggled to grow Ariel's premium share in India.
- **Campaign chapters:**
  - **2015 #ShareTheLoad** — laundry as a symbol of chore inequality; product "so good anybody could use it." Launch metric: **79% of men said laundry is a woman's job**; **73MM views, ~80% TG reach.**
  - **2016 Dads #ShareTheLoad** — children copy what they see; make fathers role models.
  - **Sons #ShareTheLoad** — mother realizes she never taught her son to share chores (daughter quitting her job under the double burden); mother as changemaker. **2020 Guinness World Record** for the largest laundry lesson (400 sons).
  - **2019 (BBDO), 4th chapter** — a little girl "untouched by social conditioning" finds her exhausted mother asleep at the washing machine; father's epiphany.
  - **2020 "#ShareTheLoad for equal sleep"** — chore inequality → **71% of Indian women sleep less than their husbands**; lockdown/WFH worsened it.
- **Agency objectives:** (a) create recall; (b) launch + buzz + start the conversation; (c) drive behavioural change via PR, events, social, influencers; (d) sustain the buzz via brand collaborations, events, pan-India reach.
- **IMC / media:** true **360°** — TV to launch each chapter → YouTube → Facebook/Twitter/Instagram to sustain → massive PR with celebrities/influencers to generate **user-generated content**. Tactics: #DateWithLaundry (Valentine's), Sesame Street / Galli Galli Sim, **#LaundryGoesOddEven** (Odd-Even Ariel pack with a chore calendar), Mommy Bloggers Meet, SON-Day, #HamperofEquality, Tinkle comic, Holi crash-courses with Whirlpool/Big Bazaar.
- **Impact metrics (Exhibit 5):**
  - *Movement:* norm shift 79% → 63% → 52%; 45,000 conversations; **98% positive/neutral sentiment**; other brands joined the cause.
  - *Business:* first-ever entry to Brand Equity's 10 Most Trusted Brands (**#7 vs. #44**); **unaided awareness +13%**, **consideration +21%**, **offtake index 125 vs. year ago**, **penetration +11%**, **share +11% vs. year ago** (growing across all states), **engagement 5x** on STL vs. non-STL posts.
  - *PR:* 45 cities activated; total PR value ~**USD 6.1M**; **~1.5 billion impressions**. WARC 2018: **World's Most Effective Campaign.**
- **The dilemma:** despite all this, **Ariel's absolute market share is not rising**. Ogilvy's maxim ("repeat your winners until they stop selling") vs. fear of wear-out and of an emotional arms race with Surf Excel's "Daag Achhe Hain." "It's not enough to create winning advertisements… the key is to capture imagination and sustain engagement for a long time."

### Central decision
- **How should Ariel evolve (refresh / extend / replace) #ShareTheLoad** so it keeps building brand equity **and converts that equity into premium-segment market share and sales** against Surf Excel — and **how should success be measured**, given that awareness, consideration, trust and social-norm metrics are all up while share (~1.4%) has barely moved?

### Key takeaways
- **Why it works:** a real cultural insight, high emotion, a brand-owned symbol ("laundry = the face of the movement"), disciplined 360° IMC (TV launch → digital sustain → PR/collab amplify → UGC), and an annual **chapter structure** that renews the story so it resists wear-out.
- **The attribution problem is the crux:** soft metrics (awareness, consideration, equity, sentiment, norm shift, ~1.5B impressions) are strongly favourable, but the hard metric — **premium-segment share** — has not moved. Likely reasons: the campaign builds *category/purpose* salience (and even helps competitors who "joined the cause"); it changes attitudes about chores, not shelf choice; distribution, price and promotion may be the binding constraints; and "+11% share" is off a tiny base.
- **Separate brand-building from activation:** hold the campaign accountable for **penetration, trial, repeat, and value/volume share in the premium segment specifically**, plus ROMI — not just buzz. Run **controlled market tests** (STL-heavy vs. control regions) to isolate the sales effect.
- **Risks of purpose marketing here:** (1) lifecycle / wear-out — how many chapters before the audience tunes out; (2) "woke-washing" scepticism if equity isn't backed by product superiority; (3) competitive cancellation — Surf's equally emotional platform; (4) the theme becoming generic as rivals adopt purpose.
- **Options for the next edition:** keep and selectively extend the winner with fresh executions but the same equity; deepen into new life-stage/workplace angles; **add a product-superiority layer** so the emotional story carries a functional reason-to-buy (the current work under-uses "so good anyone can use it"); pair with sharper retail/e-commerce activation, sampling and price-pack architecture to convert equity into trial; localise/vernacular for reach.
- **Measurement framework for the meeting:** (a) movement/social metrics — track, don't mistake for results; (b) brand-health — awareness, consideration, trust, price-premium acceptance; (c) **behavioural/business — trial, repeat, premium-segment share, offtake, ROMI (set targets here)**; (d) earned-media efficiency — PR value, engagement, cost per reach.
