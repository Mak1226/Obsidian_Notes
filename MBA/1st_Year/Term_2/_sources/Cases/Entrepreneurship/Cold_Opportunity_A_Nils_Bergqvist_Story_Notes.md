# Cold Opportunity (A): The Nils Bergqvist Story — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Cold_Opportunity_A_Nils_Bergqvist_Story.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Darden case (Saras Sarasvathy) — Part A of the ICEHOTEL trilogy and the classic **effectuation** set-up. **Nils Yngve Bergqvist**, a Swedish environmental engineer who hated his numbered existence at a Kiruna mining company, drifts into summer river-rafting tourism in Jukkasjärvi (northern Sweden), builds it up (30+ boats, ~40 staff, a restaurant), but is stuck with dead winters that locals insist can't be sold ("too cold, too dark"). Following his own love of snow and Arctic light, he chases foreign guests, meets Japanese travel contact **Sakata-san** and ice artist **Miho Aoki**, and stages an **ice-sculpture workshop** at the end of November 1989 with ~14 artists carving blocks from the frozen Torne River. On the Monday it is due to open, it **rains at +7°C and the sculptures melt** — with tourists and press pouring off the train. Bergqvist sits down to decide what to do next.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Founder's "means at hand":** identity — wanted to be a ship's captain, loves skiing/snow/cold/Northern Lights, environmentalist; knowledge — trained environmental engineer, 8 years in mining, self-taught tour operator; networks — tourist bureaus, a Swedish journalist, Sakata-san, artists, the local village.
- **How the venture grew (causally *un*planned):** one paying kayak client → "coffee money" hobby → bought one boat, quit the mine → 30+ boats, ~40 summer employees → restaurant → local tourism-industry member. First mover locally even as 30–40 competitors appeared.
- **The winter problem:** tourist trade is seasonal; the local tourist office literally closes in winter; locals say winter tourism is not "an idea."
- **Stakeholder acquisition by self-selection:** cold-called the Swedish Tourist Office → Scandinavian Tourist Board Tokyo → **Sakata-san** (Japanese, family gift-box factory, family "Bar Finland," father worked at Japan's consulate in Finland). One phone call → Sakata visits; Japanese tourists start coming; Bergqvist starts visiting Japan.
- **The ice idea, discovered not forecast:** in Hokkaido he sees ice art at his hotel, asks the desk clerk about artists, has a beer with **Miho Aoki**, invites her to Sweden.
- **The workshop & the contingency:** ~14 artists, end of Nov 1989, Jukkasjärvi; ice cut from the Torne River; heavy press and local excitement; then Monday 6 a.m. rain at **+7°C** destroys the exhibit ("the ice-art reindeer lay down").

### Central decision
- With the exhibition melting in front of him and tourists/press/locals arriving, **what should Bergqvist do?** More generally: how does an entrepreneur with no plan, no forecast and modest resources turn an ambiguous situation (and a literal disaster) into a venture?

### Key takeaways
- **Effectual, not causal, reasoning:** Bergqvist starts from *who he is, what he knows, whom he knows* and takes the next affordable step — he does not research a market, write a plan, or set a return target.
- **Affordable loss:** every move (kayak trips, first boat, the workshop) was small and self-funded ("coffee money"); he risks what he can afford to lose, not what the "opportunity" is worth.
- **Crazy-quilt:** the venture is built out of self-selected, committed stakeholders (Sakata, Aoki, journalists, the village) who each bring means and shape where it goes.
- **Lemonade principle (set up here, paid off in B):** the rain, the cold, the darkness and the impermanence are treated as *assets*, not obstacles.
- **Demand is discovered by acting:** tourists revealed what they wanted (Northern Lights, sleeping on ice) by showing up and asking — it was never predicted.
