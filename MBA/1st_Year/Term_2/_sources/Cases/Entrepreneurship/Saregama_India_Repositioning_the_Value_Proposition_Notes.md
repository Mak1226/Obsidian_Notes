# Saregama India Ltd.: Repositioning the Value Proposition — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Saregama_India_Repositioning_the_Value_Proposition.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Ivey/ISB case. May 2018. **Vikram Mehra**, MD of **Saregama India** (formerly Gramophone Company of India / HMV — 117 years old, owner of "practically every piece of music recorded in India since 1900"), has just turned a struggling B2B music-licensing company around with **Carvaan** — a ₹6,000 retro-looking portable player pre-loaded with 5,000 evergreen Hindi songs, aimed at 40+ nostalgia listeners and bought mostly by their adult children as a gift. Revenue jumped ₹2.1B → ₹3.5B (FY17→FY18), PBT ₹161M → ₹411M, and Saregama sold 95,000 units in one quarter. Mehra wants Saregama to become a "₹20 billion IP content company" in five years and must now set short- and long-term strategy: keep scaling B2C hardware (more Carvaan variants), refocus on the core B2B content/IP business, or pursue both — and in what sequence.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **The asset:** copyrighted back catalogue — ~300,000 songs, 23,000 artists, 50,000 contracts (2005 figures); the classics of Kishore Kumar, Rafi, Asha, Lata. RPG Group took over the ailing HMV in 1985; renamed Saregama (Sa-Re-Ga-Ma) in 2000; RP-Sanjiv Goenka Group (with McKinsey/BCG) set a "content production house" vision; Mehra (ex-Tata Sky CMO) became 17th MD in Oct 2014.
- **Industry shift:** Indian M&E US$27.3B (2016) → $45.1B (2021); music only ~1% of M&E; legitimate music ~1–2% of consumption (piracy history). Consumers moved to **"rental, not ownership"** — OTT (Gaana, Saavn), YouTube (1–3 paise/stream, huge volume), telecom ringback tones fading; publishing/public-performance licensing a strength for Saregama.
- **Phase 1 — consumer insight (2015 study, 23 cities):**
  1. **Access:** 40+ listeners want music "at the press of a button," control in their own hands, no dependence on children, and hate complicated apps; Carvaan plays an artist back-to-back for ~36 hours, no ads, no intervention, no internet (so no piracy/buffering).
  2. **Purpose = nostalgia + relaxation:** escape from a "high-strung," 12-button-washing-machine life.
  3. **Route to the consumer:** nuclear families → children away from home need a meaningful, non-repetitive gift for parents; retirees won't buy an "indulgence" for themselves but it's rational as a **gift**. ⇒ **user = 40+ music lover; buyer = their adult child.**
- **Phase 2 — the product (Carvaan, May 2017):** ₹6,000, ~20% margin, made in China; retro transistor form (nostalgia cue) + Bluetooth/USB/FM so grandchildren can use it too ("makes the product complete"). Playlists from Saregama's proprietary, copyrighted randomizing logic fed by monthly play-data across YouTube/streaming/telecom/TV/radio — **hard to clone** (rivals can't legally load the same songs).
  - **Carvaan Mini** (Oct 2017): ~₹2,000, positioned as a Bluetooth speaker + 251 hits, "dealer-push" product for younger buyers.
  - **Regional variants** (2018): Tamil, Bengali, Marathi; more planned.
  - **Channel redesign:** abandoned the dealer "sale-or-return" model (returns had exceeded sales and "killed the music business") for a **company-owned, cash-and-carry, no-return** network through electronics stores + Amazon + Saregama's ~7,000 outlets.
- **Financial logic (CFO Aayeer):** low per-unit margin × high volume = large absolute profit **and** cash to acquire IP (6–7 film-music titles bought "with your own money, not borrowed"); a growth product re-rated a company investors had dismissed as a low-growth B2B "annuity"; expanded releases to US/Canada/Europe/Middle East; scripts 180 → ~900 in a year.
- **Parallel bet:** **Yoodlee Films** — feature films for the young, tech-savvy audience, each budgeted to recover its cost; needs to become a repeatable model.
- **Mehra's two alternatives:** **Option A** — stay B2B: produce/acquire film + non-film music rights and distribute via network + apps; pure content/IP focus (conservative?). **Option B** — push deeper into B2C with more portable, digitally innovative Carvaan variants. Plus: phased (one segment then the other) or both simultaneously?

### Central decision
- **How should Mehra set Saregama's short- and long-term strategy** now that Carvaan works — double down on B2C hardware, refocus on core B2B content/IP licensing, or run both in parallel (and in what sequence)? And what is Carvaan's own roadmap, given it "cannot remain a one-product bundle"?

### Key takeaways
- **Repositioning ≠ new content:** the innovation was a new way to *package and deliver* a legacy catalogue to an under-served segment; the B2B→B2C stretch monetizes the same IP directly.
- **Who uses vs. who pays:** the breakout insight is a two-sided target (nostalgia-seeking parent as user, gift-giving child as buyer) — design, pricing and messaging must serve both.
- **Design straight from the insight:** every feature (no internet, fixed volume, hours of hands-off play, retro form, Bluetooth for grandkids) maps to a stated need.
- **Hardware economics:** thin unit margin but high volume = big profit and, crucially, **self-funded IP acquisition**; a growth product can re-rate a "boring annuity" company.
- **Align the channel to the new model:** replacing sale-or-return with company-owned cash-and-carry distribution removed the returns problem that had crippled the old business.
- **Defensibility:** owned catalogue + proprietary data-driven playlist logic make Carvaan hard to copy.
- **Manage one-product risk:** roadmap Carvaan (Mini, regional, export) *and* run parallel bets (Yoodlee, OTT/publishing) — and decide "platform (capital-intensive) vs. content company."
