# Zipcar: Refining the Business Model — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Zipcar_Refining_the_Business_Model.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBS case. October 14, 2000. **Robin Chase** (CEO/cofounder, MIT Sloan MBA) and **Antje Danielson** (cofounder, part-time — still at Harvard, second child due in weeks) have run **Zipcar**, an urban car-sharing service, in Boston for four months: 19 cars, ~250 members, ~$375,000 raised (all via serial convertible loans). Real operating data now contradicts several founding assumptions — utilization, attrition, lease and parking costs — and Chase has already repriced once (annual fee $300 → $75, hourly $1.50 → $4.50–7.00). She needs ~$1.3M more to prove the Boston market and must build the strongest possible case for the **Springboard 2000** venture forum at month-end: is Zipcar's revised business model economically viable, at what utilization and scale does a car (and a city) turn profitable, and is this a fundable, replicable business?

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Concept:** members pay a membership fee + hourly + per-mile; reserve any car online; cars sit in reserved neighborhood spaces (≤5-min walk); a wireless "black box" + proximity **Zipcard** unlocks the car and logs time/miles for real-time billing (patent filed). Aimed at urban drivers who log **<6,000 miles/year**; every shared car reportedly replaces ~7.5 owned cars.
- **Market:** Western Europe ~0.01% of drivers but +30%/yr; US essentially untouched (66M people in top-20 metros). Competitors mostly mission-driven (Flexcar, Portland Car-Sharing, CommunAuto); threat of Hertz/Avis or carmakers entering.
- **Original Dec 1999 plan (Exhibit 3):** $25 application + $300 refundable deposit + **$300/yr** + **$1.50/hr** + $0.40/mile; assumed **5% attrition**, **40% max utilization** (mature EU cos cap at 50% / 360 hrs per month), avg member 4 trips/month × 4 hrs × 22 miles.
- **Revised model (from ~May 2000, Exhibit 5):** annual fee → **$75**; hourly → **$4.50–$7.00** (by area parking cost), avg assumed **$5.50/hr**; new **$44/day cap** (undercut rental cars, attract daily renters); **attrition → 15%/yr** (Boston turnover); **lease $4,400 → $4,800/car/yr** ("bigger credit risk once we leased 10–12"); **parking not free → ~$600–750/car/yr**; fuel ~10% over plan; access equipment → $500/car/yr.
- **Overhead:** ~$44,000/month total — ~$30,000 city-independent **corporate overhead** (legal, web, tech, design, marketing materials) + ~$14,000 **Boston-specific**. Marketing kept to ~$1,000–1,500/month (~$7,300 total) — 30–40% word of mouth, 25% free PR, rest guerrilla (postcards at parking spots/subway stops); deliberately no brochure ("if they can't handle the Web, they're not our members").
- **Financing:** $50,000 angel "convertible loan" (accretes 1%/month, converts at Series A) → serial notes to **$375,000** by Sept 2000; founders take no salary. Launched June 22, 2000 with a "primitive solution" (any card opens any car, keys in glove box, paper logs, monthly manual data collection) rather than wait for the full platform.
- **Mis-hire:** brought in a "big-company guy" as president; his procedures were "25% out of date by the time they hit my desk, 50% the next day"; severed in July 2000 ("the wrong guy… cut our losses as quickly as possible").
- **Progress / need:** 19 cars (more than needed, for distribution/network density), ~250 members, demand strong enough to add cars. Needs **~$1.3M** to finish tech, prove the model and understand demand. Boston profile pool ~15,000 people; target ≥10,000 by year 5; concept replicable in ≥14 US cities; strong within-market network effects. **Springboard 2000** pitch due end of October.

### Central decision
- **Rework Zipcar's business model and unit economics** with real Boston data: can a single car — and the Boston market — reach profitability, at what utilization and fleet scale, given the higher costs and attrition and the repriced tariff? And **build the funding case**: is Zipcar a fundable, scalable venture, and what milestones would convince a Springboard investor to put in ~$1.3M?

### Key takeaways
- **Original vs. revised model:** the low-fee, low-hourly "cheaper than owning" pricing didn't cover costs; Zipcar repriced *upward* toward (but below) rental-car rates and toward heavier users / daily renters, and marked attrition and lease/parking costs sharply higher.
- **Per-car economics to build:** fixed cost/car ≈ lease ~$4,800 + parking ~$750 + insurance + equipment ~$500 (+ allocated overhead); **break-even utilization = fixed cost per car ÷ (contribution per hour × available hours)**. At ~$5.50/hr and ~40% utilization (~175 hrs/month ≈ $960/car/month) a car covers its direct costs — so viability hinges on spreading the ~$30k/month corporate overhead across a large fleet and multiple cities.
- **Scale + density + first-mover:** more cars → shorter walk → more members → more cars; being first to build density in a market is a real advantage, and the city-independent tech/corporate layer needs multi-city volume to pay off.
- **Value proposition:** vs. ownership (no purchase/insurance/maintenance/parking for low-mileage drivers) and vs. rental (hourly, neighborhood, 24/7 self-service) — but the repricing narrows the gap to rental, so **convenience and location** are the durable differentiators.
- **Is it fundable?** Pros: demonstrated demand, very low CAC, defensible patented tech, a replicable single-market playbook. Cons: cash burned on a mis-hire, dilution from stacked convertible notes, a part-time absentee co-founder, and no proven per-market or blended profitability yet. Investor milestones: Boston hitting a target member count / utilization / positive local operating margin, validated unit economics, and a credible multi-city rollout cost curve.
- **Founder lessons:** hire for your current stage; priced rounds beat serial notes; launch with the "bare minimum" and iterate; co-founder commitment and role clarity matter.
