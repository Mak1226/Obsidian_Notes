# Dhamani Jewels: Becoming a Global Luxury Brand — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Dhamani_Jewels_Becoming_a_Global_Luxury_Brand.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBS case (Applegate & Mazzanti). Summer 2014, Dubai. **Amit Dhamani** (CEO) and the Dhamani family — a business that began in 1969 with his father **L.N. Dhamani** as a traveling salesman of loose cut emeralds from Jaipur, then added rubies/sapphires (Bangkok), diamonds, jewelry manufacturing, and finally Dubai retail from 2000 — are converting their Dubai Mall store into **Dhamani 1969**, a $2.5M, art-gallery-style, fixed-price flagship next to Cartier and Bulgari, designed by Callison. It is the foundation of a stated vision to become an **iconic global luxury jewelry brand**. The family must decide whether that vision is feasible and desirable, how to sequence it (GCC regional rollout via Qatar, or leap straight to London), whether to keep investing in Dubai hotel/resort stores and close the souk stores, and what governance and outside-capital changes the family business needs.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **The family firm:** executive team = L.N. (Chairman), Pushpa (Director), and three brothers with defined roles — **Amit** (CEO), **Rohit** (Wholesale & Manufacturing), **Manish** (Retail) — plus all three wives as directors: **Nupur** (finance/control), **Preeti** (manufacturing), **Priya** (marketing/brand). 2012: 165 employees.
- **Growth by "inflection points":** 1969 loose emeralds (Jaipur) → late-1970s go abroad → Bangkok office (rubies + sapphires + emeralds) → 1993 jewelry manufacturing → 2000 diamonds (certified via GIA/labs; a client buying $100k of colored stones buys ~$1M of diamonds) → **1998 Dubai office** (1/3 of world within a 4-hr flight; tax/duty-free; tourist hub) → **2000 first retail store** (Gold Souk) → 2004–05 GDP, Al Maktoum St, then first **mall store** at Mall of the Emirates → HQ moved to Dubai → post-2008 crash pivot from real-estate-mogul clients to **tourists** → 2012+ **luxury hotel/resort stores** (Emirates Towers, Abu Dhabi's Emirates Palace, Palm Island, Muscat).
- **Vertical integration = margin capture (Exhibit 2):** rough gems 5% → cutting/polishing 10% → polished sales 10% → manufacturing 10% → **retail 10–25%**. Dhamani spans the whole chain for colored stones (cutting-to-retail for diamonds); ~25–30% price edge vs. retailers who buy loose stones + finished jewelry. Retail = 35% gross profit vs. wholesale 15%; revenue ~50/50 but 70% of exec time on retail.
- **Brand assets built:** first recipient of Dubai's **Bareeq** jewelry certification (2003); **exclusive license for the Dubai Cut Diamond** (99 facets; won 2006 with a letter-"D" design proposal, surprising for a young brand); hired **FITCH** (brand strategy — brand standards, tone of voice, brand map, aspirational competitor set: Cartier, Bulgari, Graff, Chopard); hired a process consultant (KPIs, balanced scorecard, Six Sigma); **Laviere of London** — a separate brand sold only wholesale to Western high-end retailers, used as a learning vehicle for London/NY/HK tastes.
- **Why "global luxury brand" is hard:** branded jewelry earns a premium at identical specs (Bain: ~40% for a Cartier ring) — "this business is about emotions" — but Dhamani's brand "isn't there yet" on markups (no Panthère/Serpenti equivalent). Interbrand 2008: Cartier #6 / Tiffany #7 ($4.2B each), Bulgari #10 ($3.3B); iconic traits = authenticity, quality commitment, substitute-proof status, **≥30% of revenue outside the origin country**; icons took 150+ years; investors avoid new luxury-brand ventures.
- **Dhamani 1969:** 1,700 sq ft Dubai Mall flagship (art-gallery concept — browse freely, expert advice, buy or commission), $2.5M build-out by Callison (did Apple/Tiffany/Bloomingdale's), same contractor as Cartier's stores, per-location design fee, **fixed prices** (a first for Dhamani). Near-term = pilot in Dubai, then a planned Dhamani 1969 in **Qatar** (~20% lower rent, higher revenue) and possibly a second in **Nakheel Mall** on Palm Island.
- **Open questions the family is debating:** delay GCC expansion and go **directly to London** ("a London store = you're in the league"), or via an Asian hub (Singapore/Hong Kong) first? Keep investing in low-rent Dubai hotel/resort stores (feeder / brand-awareness, not profit centers)? Close the souk stores (already decided — as leases expire)? An **incentive bonus** for mall/hotel staff who refer clients to Dhamani 1969? Keep **wholesale** (yes — it funds growth and balances retail cyclicality; target retail 50% → 70% of revenue). Bring in **outside equity** only after the concept is proven; what board/governance is needed once ownership goes beyond the family?

### Central decision
- **Is the vision of an iconic global luxury brand feasible and desirable — and how should Dhamani sequence it?** After the Dubai Mall pilot: regional GCC rollout (Qatar) + Palm Island first to learn cheaply, or leap to a Western luxury capital (London, possibly via Singapore/Hong Kong)? And what changes to the family business — professionalization, governance, outside capital, and the "live and work together" model — are required to support it?

### Key takeaways
- **What the vision requires that Dhamani lacks:** a distinctive iconic identity and signature designs, ≥30% of sales outside the home region, decades of heritage money can't buy fast, and patient capital. **What it has:** vertical-integration margin, a genuine quality/trust reputation, the Dubai Cut Diamond exclusive, a tourist-hub location, and a capable, aligned family.
- **Positioning wedge:** don't copy Cartier/Bulgari — offer "comparable-to-the-top product with more personalization and a better price without sacrificing quality," plus the gallery/commission experience and the "colors of luxury" (emerald/ruby/sapphire) sourcing edge; fixed pricing and Callison design are credibility signals.
- **Expansion sequencing trade-off:** cheap regional learning (Qatar/Palm) and an Asia hub vs. the brand-signaling value and urgency of a marquee London address (regional stores teach little about Western taste — that's Laviere's job).
- **Family-business professionalization → governance:** they've added strategists, consultants, scorecards and defined roles; the next step is a real board, formal decision rights, a family constitution, and a plan for how the co-located husband-wife model survives global scaling.
- **Outside capital, sequenced:** prove the model and build a track record first, then take aligned investors — premature equity risks the vision and control that make Dhamani distinctive.
- **Prune the portfolio without abandoning the base:** keep wholesale (funds and de-risks, reads trends), close off-brand souk stores as leases lapse, keep hotel stores as feeders — and accept that some channel conflict is the price of building a retail brand.
