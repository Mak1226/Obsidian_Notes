# Intrapreneurship at Alcatel-Lucent — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Intrapreneurship_at_Alcatel_Lucent.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Ivey case. June 2014. **Olivier Leclerc**, Director of Open Innovation & Intrapreneurship at **Alcatel-Lucent (ALU)** — a loss-making French telecom-equipment maker in a commoditizing, consolidating industry — is preparing to **relaunch the company's intrapreneurship "boot camps."** They ran 2006–2012 (Belgium first, then France/China/India/US) and were killed during a €1.25B cost-cutting program, but a new CEO's **Shift Plan** wants "innovation back at the heart of Alcatel-Lucent." Leclerc faces three dilemmas: how to reconcile a scale-driven, **failure-averse** corporation with **failure-prone** internal venturing; how to build a forward-looking element linked to core businesses; and how to redesign the boot camp's architecture — idea sourcing, mid-manager buy-in, metrics/rewards, careers for intrapreneurs, exit/IP rules, and what to do with participants who don't win.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **The programme:** "Innovation Boot Camp" (Belgium, 2006) → "Entrepreneurial Boot Camp" (2008, extended to France/China/India/US) → French edition "**Défi entreprendre**." Three goals: capture value from employee ideas, stimulate entrepreneurial spirit, develop people. Mechanics: volunteer + idea → multi-disciplinary team (design, mfg, marketing, customer + originator) → weekend retreats + B-school curriculum → "Super Friday" pitch to a jury (incl. a CEO and local VCs) on market need, financial return **and vision of value for ALU** → incubation with customers → launch under an experienced manager.
- **Why it was killed (end 2012):** falling revenue, rising cost of sales, the **Performance Program** (€1.25B savings; −5,500 employees, −1,400 contractors, exit unprofitable units); a view that lean sub-scale businesses "didn't fit" a scale/process-driven firm.
- **Why it's being revived:** Feb 2013 new CEO replaced Performance Program with the **Shift Plan** — reposition to cash-generative IP-networking/cloud/broadband segments; three businesses (Core Networking, Access, Others); "put innovation back at the heart."
- **Landing options for a project:** transfer to a business unit, hand to **Alcatel-Lucent Ventures** (Bell Labs incubator), or spin off via external VC. ALU **preferred to own** the idea.
- **Défi entreprendre results (to end 2012, France):** 32 projects → **2 commercialized** (MOSAR/Alternative Energy — solar/wind base stations, €20M revenue 2009–11; Dekaps — geo-localized multimedia software, €1M), **3 transferred to BUs** (Contact Me My Way, SCANER, Tivizio), **1 spun off** (Broadcast over Cellular — the 3 employees left with the IP; **venture shut down 2014** — "without the brand equity of Alcatel-Lucent, not sustainable").
- **Three insights to build on:**
  1. **Ideas/people did not mainly come from R&D:** Marketing **40%**, R&D 33%, Project Mgmt 12%, Sales 7%, Legal 4%, Finance 3%, HR 1%.
  2. Wide age mix (peak 36% at 35–40; substantial 40–50).
  3. Boot camps raised "high-potential" (HiPo) employees from **48% → 66%** of the workforce.
- **Unresolved limitations:** mid-level managers won't free up resources (tried a "least expectation"/non-obstruction rule); trainees fear it's a prelude to layoffs; **no plan for participants who don't win**; hard to define performance **metrics** and tie them to KPIs and rewards.
- **What Leclerc wants:** a "seamless flow between the strategic needs of start-ups and of Alcatel-Lucent" — every project linked to a core business; C-level managers as mentors; year-round boot camps; open to all employees, the vendor community, maybe outside firms; a proper **exit plan** and clear HR/IP rules; an **atypical career path** so intrapreneurs stay (most preferred to stay); **activity-based metrics that reward innovation/learning, not just outcomes**.
- **Context:** telecom-equipment industry ~US$351B (2013), commoditizing, Asian low-price entrants + IT firms (IBM/HP/Accenture) eroding incumbents. ALU: €14.4B revenue, >15% on R&D, 32,000+ patents, Bell Labs, net losses 2009/10/12/13.

### Central decision
- **How should Leclerc redesign and relaunch the boot camps under the Shift Plan?** Specifically: (1) reconcile failure-averse scale/efficiency culture with failure-prone venturing; (2) add a forward-looking element tied to core-business strategy; (3) fix the architecture — source ideas beyond R&D, win mid-manager buy-in, define innovation-appropriate metrics/rewards, build a career track and exit/IP rules that retain intrapreneurs, and decide the fate of non-winning participants and projects.

### Key takeaways
- **Programme design:** open idea sourcing across all functions (Marketing, not R&D, was the top source), staged funding gates, dedicated multi-disciplinary teams, a jury/board, and pre-defined landing options (BU, internal incubator, external spin-off).
- **Beat the organizational antibodies:** firms built for scale, standardization and incremental efficiency treat sub-scale ventures as misfits and mid-managers hoard resources — counter with C-suite sponsorship, non-obstruction rules, and by linking every project to a core-business need so it's not seen as a drain.
- **Incentives/careers for intrapreneurs:** they want autonomy, risk and recognition, not equity upside and not necessarily a GM ladder — needs a dedicated track, process/activity-based metrics that reward the experiment, and protection from the "prep for layoffs" fear.
- **Spin in / spin out / kill:** ALU's own spin-off failed without the parent's brand, customers and scale — the corporate assets *are* the intrapreneur's advantage, so default to keeping ideas in-house.
- **Measure the right thing:** only 2 of 32 projects were commercialized, yet the programme lifted HiPo share 48%→66% — value shows up in talent and pipeline, not only P&L.
