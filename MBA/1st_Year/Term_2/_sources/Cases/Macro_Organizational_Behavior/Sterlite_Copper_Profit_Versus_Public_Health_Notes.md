# Sterlite Copper: Profit Versus Public Health — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Sterlite_Copper_Profit_Versus_Public_Health.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Ivey case (Veena Keshav Pailwar). May 28, 2018. The Government of Tamil Nadu orders the **permanent shutdown** of **Sterlite Copper** — a ~400,000-tonne/year smelter in Tuticorin owned by **Vedanta Limited** (part of Vedanta Resources) that meets ~⅓ of India's copper demand — after a 100-day protest against a proposed capacity doubling turned violent and **police firing killed at least 11 people** on May 22. Local residents blame two decades of air and groundwater pollution for cancer and respiratory disease; Vedanta denies it, blaming nearby thermal plants for 99% of the region's SO₂. Sterlite is 5% of Vedanta's EBITDA and ~3% of Tamil Nadu's GDP, with ~4,000 direct and ~20,000 downstream jobs. The class question: **what should Vedanta do — challenge the closure at the National Green Tribunal / Supreme Court, relocate to another state, or accept it — and what stakeholder and reputation strategy should it adopt?**

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Strategic value:** ~400 kt/yr refined copper (~1/3 of Indian demand); ~55% domestic, rest exported; supplies 400+ downstream SMEs; by-products (sulphuric acid, phosphoric acid, copper slag, anode slime). Annual sales ₹220B; **5% of Vedanta EBITDA** (was 14.6% in 2015-16, Exhibit 6); **~3% of Tamil Nadu GDP**; saves ~₹140B of import forex; ~4,000 direct jobs (70% from TN), ~20,000 indirect. India imports ~100% of copper concentrate (only ~2% of world ore reserves).
- **The expansion (the spark):** Vedanta wanted to double capacity 0.4 → 0.8 Mt via a second SIPCOT unit; had 2009 environmental clearance and 2016 permission; had committed ₹20B of ₹35B and begun hiring. The Feb 2018 doubling announcement reignited protests.
- **A 20-year conflict:**
  - Maharashtra (Ratnagiri) withdrew Vedanta's permission in 1993 under public pressure; Gujarat and Goa also refused. Tamil Nadu granted permission within a year.
  - Permit condition: locate **≥25 km from the Gulf of Mannar**; the plant was built **within 14 km** — a violation TN "ignored" because Sterlite was nominally zero-discharge, licensing it in Oct 1996.
  - **1998 NEERI study** (court-ordered) found groundwater contaminated with **arsenic, lead, selenium** and gas leaks sickening nearby workers → Madras HC ordered closure; the company got it modified; litigation ran 10+ years.
  - Sept 2010 HC shutdown order → Supreme Court stay → **April 2013 SC overturned the shutdown** ("substantial contribution to copper production… defence, electricity, infrastructure… ~1,300 employees plus contractors") but **fined Vedanta ₹1 billion** for violating environmental norms.
  - 2013: gas-leak complaints (coughing, eye irritation, miscarriages) → TNPCB shutdown → **National Green Tribunal ordered reopening** ("no scientific data supporting emissions in excess of prescribed parameters").
  - Feb–May 2018: protests grew from 250 people; plant shut in March for "maintenance"; TNPCB refused reopening; NGOs, political parties, and the international Tamil diaspora / UK Labour (which called Vedanta "a rogue company" and demanded LSE delisting) mobilized; **May 22 police firing, ≥11 dead** → permanent shutdown.
- **Vedanta's group reputation:** BALCO Chhattisgarh (2009 chimney collapse, guilty inquiry "suppressed"); Niyamgiri Odisha (tribes rejected bauxite mining); Zambia (Konkola Copper Mines bought undervalued, transfer-pricing profit-hiding, Kafue River acid pollution).
- **Sterlite's defence:** invested >₹5B in abatement (flue-gas desulphurization, reverse osmosis, evaporators); 4th-lowest energy-use copper smelter globally; zero-liquid-discharge since inception; 68-hectare green cover; CDP reporting; British Safety Council "five star." **The science:** many government/independent studies found severe air and water pollution but **could not attribute it to Sterlite specifically**; a 2006-07 Tirunelveli Medical College study found high respiratory-infection prevalence in a 5-km radius attributable to air pollution but "not strongly" to Sterlite, and found no arsenic in groundwater.
- **CEO P. Ramnath:** "A total turnover of ₹600 billion is affected… False propaganda dominated over the truth… we will explore all options."

### Central decision
- **What should Vedanta do?** Fight the permanent-shutdown order at the National Green Tribunal / Supreme Court, **relocate** the plant to another (willing) state, or **accept closure** — and what stakeholder-engagement and reputation strategy should it adopt after 20 years of pollution allegations, court battles, a permit violation, and a lost "social licence to operate"?

### Key takeaways
- **Map the stakeholders and their colliding claims:** local community (health, clean air/water, voice, compensation); ~24,000 direct + downstream workers (livelihoods); Tamil Nadu (revenue and import substitution vs. political fallout from 11 deaths); central government and copper-using industries (supply security — Sterlite = ⅓ of Indian demand); NGOs / activists / diaspora (environmental justice); Vedanta shareholders (EBITDA, ₹600B turnover, share-price hit). "Profit vs. public health" is the label; **trust and legitimacy** are the real issue.
- **Where the social licence was lost:** building 14 km from the Gulf of Mannar against the 25-km condition; a documented NEERI finding of arsenic/lead/selenium contamination and worker sickness; two decades of leaks, complaints and shutdown-reopening cycles in which the company was repeatedly "exonerated" on narrow attribution grounds while residents' lived experience said otherwise; and a group-wide pattern (Niyamgiri, BALCO, Zambia) of corner-cutting and suppressed findings. Denials, blame-shifting to thermal plants, and "false propaganda" rhetoric **deepened distrust** instead of rebuilding it.
- **Where it could have been prevented:** at siting (honour the buffer); at licensing (don't seek exemptions for violations); across 20 years of complaints (independent, transparent, community-governed emissions and health monitoring; substantive grievance redress; visibly fix leaks); and above all at the **expansion decision** — a fresh participatory environmental and social impact assessment and community consent *before* committing ₹20B and hiring. Once protests were peaceful for 100 days, engagement — not police force — was the off-ramp.
- **"Profit vs. public health" is a false binary:** the science was genuinely ambiguous *and* the company had spent ₹5B on abatement. A workable model: co-designed independent monitoring with real community authority, transparent data, a health/remediation fund regardless of attribution, relocation away from the population and the Gulf of Mannar buffer, and treating consent as **continuing**, not a one-time permit.
- **Legal win vs. durable operations:** Vedanta could likely prevail again at the NGT/SC on "no proven excess emissions + national importance + jobs" — but reopening against an unwilling state and a mobilised community is permanent conflict, not an operating basis. Relocation resets the licence but is slow, costly, and other states have already refused Vedanta. Accepting closure protects the group brand but sacrifices a strategic asset and copper self-sufficiency.
- **Managing environmentally contentious operations:** obtain and continuously renew community consent; site with generous, honoured buffers; make monitoring independent and community-governed; answer the first complaints substantively, not legally; don't expand before re-securing consent; invest visibly in local health and economy; and never let a dispute escalate to force — by then the licence to operate is already gone.
