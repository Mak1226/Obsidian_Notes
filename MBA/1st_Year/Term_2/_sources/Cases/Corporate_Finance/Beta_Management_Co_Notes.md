# Beta Management Co. — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Beta_Management_Co.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> **Sarah Wolfe**, founder/CEO of Beta Management Group (a Boston-area money manager, ~$25M AUM, high-net-worth clients, founded 1988), runs a market-timing strategy that parks 50–99% of assets in the **Vanguard Index 500 Trust** and the rest in money-market instruments. To answer prospective clients who dislike pure indexing — and to lift equity exposure to 80% — she is considering adding **one** small, beaten-down stock: **California REIT** (~$2.25 on 4 Jan 1991) or **Brown Group** (footwear; ~$24). A $200,000 buy would take total equity to $20M, so the stock is ~1% of the portfolio. Using 24 months of returns (Jan 1989–Dec 1990, Table 1), students compute each stock's standalone volatility and its beta vs. the index, and discover that the more volatile stock can *lower* total portfolio risk if its correlation with the market is low.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Firm:** Beta Management Group, ~$25M AUM; strategy = adjust equity exposure 50–99% via one low-cost index fund (Vanguard Index 500 Trust), rest in MMF. Lagged the market in 1989; beat it in 1990 by cutting equity to 50% in June before a decline, then rebuilding to **79.2%** by 4 Jan 1991.
- **Motivation to change:** lost prospective individual and small-institutional clients who found "index-only, picks no stocks" unusual; New Year's resolution to start buying individual (small-cap) stocks and raise equity to 80%.
- **Candidate 1 — California REIT:** equity/mortgage REIT in AZ/CA/WA income property; hammered by the 1989 "World Series" earthquake and the California real-estate downturn; **extremely volatile** standalone; price $2¼.
- **Candidate 2 — Brown Group:** large branded-footwear maker/retailer (Jordache, Naturalizer, Buster Brown); restructuring since 1989; steady positive earnings; price seen as variable and **market-sensitive**; price $24.
- **Data:** Table 1 = monthly returns for the index fund, California REIT, Brown Group, Jan 1989–Dec 1990 (24 obs). Figures 1 & 2 plot each stock vs. the index.
- **Transaction size:** $200,000 in one stock → total equity exposure $20M (stock ≈ 1% weight).
- **Constraint / promise:** Wolfe has always promised clients "reasonable returns with risk under control."

### Central decision
- Should Wolfe add **California REIT**, **Brown Group**, or neither to the (almost entirely index-fund) equity portfolio — and how should she judge the risk each one adds?
- Underlying question: standalone risk (return SD) vs. **contribution to portfolio risk** (covariance/beta with the market). For a client already holding the market, only systematic risk should matter.

### Key takeaways
- **Rough numbers (monthly, 24 obs):** California REIT SD ≈ 9.2%, Brown Group SD ≈ 8.2%, index SD ≈ 4.6%. But **beta**: California REIT ≈ 0.14 (near-zero correlation with the market), Brown Group ≈ 1.16.
- **Diversification beats "low volatility":** adding ~1% California REIT *reduces* the portfolio's standard deviation despite it being the most volatile stock alone, because its returns barely move with the index. Adding Brown Group slightly *raises* portfolio risk.
- **Right metric = beta / covariance, not standalone SD**, once the investor is already diversified. This is the CAPM intuition in miniature.
- **Recommendation:** on risk grounds, California REIT is the better addition; the case also invites debate on whether a tiny 1% position is even worth the effort vs. Wolfe's real problem (a concentrated, single-fund, market-timing strategy and client communication).
- **Caveats:** only 24 monthly observations; California REIT's thin trading and stale prices bias its beta downward; past co-movement need not persist.
