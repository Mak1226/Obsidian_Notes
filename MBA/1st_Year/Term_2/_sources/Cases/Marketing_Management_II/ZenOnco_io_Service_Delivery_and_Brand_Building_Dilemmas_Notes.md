# ZenOnco.io: Service Delivery and Brand-Building Dilemmas — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[ZenOnco_io_Service_Delivery_and_Brand_Building_Dilemmas.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Ivey case (Mathew, Puri, Chopra). Mid-July 2022. **Dimple Parmar** (CEO) and **Kishan Shah** (CA/CFA co-founder) run **ZenOnco.io** — an **"integrative oncology"** platform founded Nov 2019 that gives end-to-end cancer-care guidance (evidence-based complementary care alongside conventional treatment) to patients across all income levels, from a for-profit model that grew out of Parmar's non-profit "Love Heals Cancer." Three years in, ZenOnco is still stuck in the start-up stage with **low brand awareness**, and its service delivery keeps failing (wrong patient–provider matches, partner-capacity gaps, undertrained counsellors, feedback that doesn't improve anything, incomplete patient journeys). Parmar and Shah set a three-point agenda: **popularize the brand**, make ZenOnco the **preferred free destination for scientific advice and counselling**, and **fix service delivery** — against 10+ funded competitors and the need to keep BPL patients' access affordable.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Origin:** Parmar's partner died of cancer (2018); she quit banking on an "inner calling," was mentored by Dr. Michael Lerner (Commonweal, *Choices in Healing*), started the non-profit **Love Heals Cancer (LHC)** for BPL patients. LHC failed to scale — donation-dependent, no stable funds, free services perceived as low quality, couldn't customize — so ZenOnco was founded (Nov 2019) as a **for-profit, business-model-driven** firm.
- **India oncology context:** ~**70% of Indian patients diagnosed at advanced stages**; doctor–patient ratio ~1:2,000; need ≥5,000 oncologists / 550 centres vs. ~1,250 / 250; cancer mortality 4–6× the US; oncologists too stretched to counsel patients. Oncology market to grow **$734M 2020–25 at 13% CAGR**; 26.7M → 29.8M patients (2021→2025).
- **Positioning: "integrative oncology"** — deliberately **not "alternative"** (which means abandoning conventional medicine); integrative = complement allopathic treatment with **scientifically backed** protocols. "io" in the name signals it. **No segmentation** — "ZenOnco for all people with cancer" (Parmar rejected advice to focus). Credibility with doctors depends entirely on **scientific validity** (research-agency licensing); oncologist referrals are "a trickle, not a flow."
- **The ZenOnco system (Exhibit 1), 6 elements:** patient counsellors; external service-facilitator collaborations; technology apps; support community; doctors/hospitals; diagnostic labs/pharma/pharmacies.
- **Service failures:** improper patient–provider matching; partners can't deliver the right service at the right time (technical/resource gaps); counsellors under-trained for patient concerns; feedback failures → no process improvement; patients not completing the service journey.
- **Six success metrics (Exhibit 2):** clinical outcome (QoL survey); **NPS**; **conversion rate** (first-time paid / new patients per month); referral rate & percentage; retention rate & organic-leads percentage; website visits (MoM growth).
- **Tools:** **Ziopar** — AI preliminary integrative-oncology assessment report from a 6-point online form; **ESMO-recognized (Sept 2020)**; 21,000+ patients in 63 countries by 2020. **4-step counselling tool** (educate → match to expert → validate that the patient used the service → monitor/feedback; the doctor still decides). **CANNECT app** (June 2021) — verified, moderated patient/survivor/expert community, positioned against fake-info-ridden Facebook/WhatsApp groups.
- **Network:** 200+ super-specialized cancer hospitals, 1,000+ oncologists; still high treatment dropout (stigma + cost), low screening awareness, specialist shortage.
- **Brand:** name = "Zen" + "oncology"; pastel green/blue crossed-ribbon logo (Exhibit 3); shifted from a "sales approach" to "offering what patients search for — information and tips." Exploring **cancer survivors as brand ambassadors and peer counsellors** (win-win). "Brand awareness and recall had to be revved up."
- **Competitors:** **Onco.com** (2017, VC-funded, one-stop, 1,500 oncologists, 18 countries); **Hospido** (2020, AI chatbot "Cancer Dost" teleconsult); **DCode Care** (2019, price-discovery platform with 20–37.5% discounts). Shared traits: digitized platform, small-but-growing scale, non-profit → for-profit, founder-driven. **ZenOnco's differentiator:** Parmar's personal loss + the founders' business/finance qualifications; and the genuinely *integrative* + community + counselling layer (not just an oncologist marketplace or price aggregator).
- **The three brand-positioning questions:** build the brand on **science**? on **emotion** (people's point of view)? or is the **integrative** approach itself the positioning? Whichever, awareness/recall must accelerate urgently.

### Central decision
- **How should Parmar and Shah (1) build ZenOnco's brand** — choosing its positioning (science-led vs. emotion-led vs. "integrative oncology" as the platform), whether to keep serving all segments or focus, and how to raise awareness and recall fast and credibly in an emotionally charged, unfamiliar category — **and (2) fix the service-delivery failures** so ZenOnco can scale profitably out of the start-up stage against 10+ funded rivals, while keeping BPL patients' access to affordable care and drugs?

### Key takeaways
- **Positioning is not either/or:** lead with **science + evidence** as the reason-to-believe (research licensing, ESMO-recognized Ziopar, oncologist endorsements) to win referrals and trust in a category prone to quackery scepticism; use the **emotional "offer of hope"** and Parmar's founding story as the human layer; **"integrative oncology" is the category descriptor**. If a single primary axis is forced, science-led is the safer anchor.
- **"Serve all" as mission, "segment" as go-to-market:** undifferentiated service is exactly what broke LHC. A **tiered model** — paid counselling/coordination/curation for middle-class and wealthy patients that **cross-subsidizes** free/low-cost access for BPL patients — keeps the mission and makes the economics sustainable.
- **Services-marketing (gaps model) diagnosis:** wrong provider matching + partner-capacity shortfalls = service-delivery gap; under-trained counsellors = a people/competency gap; feedback that doesn't change anything = a learning gap; incomplete journeys = a handoff/participation gap. Fixes: standardize and supervise the counsellor role with training and protocols; put **SLAs and capacity visibility** into the hospital/oncologist network; instrument the 4-step process and the Exhibit-2 metrics so feedback actually drives change; redesign the patient journey (Exhibit 4) with clear handoffs and follow-up to cut dropout.
- **Brand-building that fits a sensitive category:** be the trusted free information source patients search for (SEO, Ziopar as a lead magnet, guideline-based content vs. social-media misinformation); use clinical partnerships and third-party validation as credibility; deploy **survivor ambassadors / peer counsellors** (authentic, scalable, and they populate CANNECT); make **CANNECT** a verified, well-moderated community as a genuine differentiator; keep a consistent identity — and avoid a hard sales pitch, which reads as exploitative in cancer care.
- **Competitive wedge:** don't try to out-scale the VC-funded one-stop players (Onco.com) or the price aggregators (DCode) — lean into *integrative* (evidence-based complementary + conventional + hope), the founder's lived experience, and the community + counselling layer.
- **Next-12-months priorities:** raise brand awareness/recall (content + survivors + partnerships), convert the oncologist referral trickle into a flow (science credibility + hospital partnerships), make counsellor/provider delivery reliable, grow paid conversion and retention (Exhibit 2), and formalize the BPL cross-subsidy so mission and margin coexist.
