# Serum Institute of India: COVID-19 Vaccine Pricing — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Serum_Institute_of_India_COVID_19_Vaccine_Pricing.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Ivey/SPJIMR case. August 6, 2020, Pune. **Adar Poonawala**, CEO of the **Serum Institute of India (SII)** — the world's largest vaccine maker (1.5B+ doses in 2019, sold in 170+ countries, ⅔ of the world's children immunized with an SII vaccine) — two weeks ago announced a licence with AstraZeneca to manufacture the Oxford COVID-19 vaccine as **"Covishield"** in India, at **US$13/dose ($26 for the two-dose course)**, with reductions "as economies of scale are realized." That price is now under attack: J&J has just announced a **$10/dose not-for-profit** US supply deal and Russia says it will mass-distribute a vaccine by October. Poonawala must decide whether to hold $13, cut it, or restructure the pricing — while recovering a **$100M new facility plus $200M+ of at-risk pre-approval production**, honouring a GAVI/Gates commitment to supply low- and middle-income countries affordably, and managing deep uncertainty about efficacy, durability and next-generation entrants.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **The $13 rationale:** SII's routine vaccines are far cheaper (measles ~$0.50/dose vs. ~$15 Western; Exhibit 2B: SII MMR $2.85, DTP $0.18). The $13 is set to recover the **$100M plant** (1B doses/yr capacity) and **200M doses produced at SII's own risk before approval** (opex >$100M). Reference points: Pfizer $19.50/dose, Moderna **$32–37**, Remdesivir >$3,000/course, a COVID test >$20; a US pediatric vaccine can be $143–$187 (Prevnar/Gardasil, Exhibit 2A).
- **Buyer segments by ability to pay (Exhibit 1):**
  - **High income** (GNI >$12,356; 1.2B people; US representative): US doesn't regulate vaccine pricing; per-capita discretionary health budget ~$3,412.
  - **Upper-middle** (GNI $4,046–12,535; 2.9B; China): routine vaccines $0.10–$6 public, up to **$100 private**.
  - **Lower-middle** (GNI $1,036–4,045; 2.7B; India): India per-capita **health budget <$10**, but 5th-largest economy with an investment-grade rating; largest vaccine production capacity in the world (3B doses, 2B exported).
  - **Low income** (GNI <$1,035; 0.6B; Ethiopia): discretionary budget ~$70/person; vaccine prices typically ~10% of US/Europe.
  - **June 2020:** SII + Gates Foundation + CEPI + GAVI + AstraZeneca deal to supply low/lower-middle-income countries affordably; Poonawala pledged **half of Indian capacity** to those countries "at an affordable and accessible price for all."
- **Competitive pricing models:**
  - **Pfizer** ($19.50/dose): "broad access" pricing during the pandemic → **"value-based"** pricing once COVID becomes standard/seasonal; declined US development funding to avoid price negotiations.
  - **Moderna** ($32–37, lower for volume): took $955M US funding but "cannot price at cost — needs returns for investors."
  - **AstraZeneca:** **not-for-profit for the duration of the pandemic**, royalties on commercial pricing after; licensed to SII, Kangtai, others.
  - **J&J:** **$10/dose not-for-profit** during the pandemic, for-profit after.
  - **Russia (Gamaleya):** "motive neither profit nor politics but humanistic"; mass vaccination from Oct 2020.
- **SII's model & economics:** privately held → takes a **long-term view**; low overhead, automation, motivated workforce → **>40% margins despite low prices** (Exhibit 3: 2019 revenue **$750M**, EBITDA $357M, 48%). Does **not develop new vaccines** — improves yields and thermostability, uses off-patent products, and **licenses** newer ones (AstraZeneca, Novavax); a departure — also developing its own COVID vaccine by modifying an off-patent one.
- **Global capacity:** ~3.5B doses/yr for all vaccines, plans to double to 4B+ COVID doses by 2021 — still a shortfall since most need two doses.
- **The decision (case):** follow Pfizer's two-part "broad access → value-based" model? Account for next-gen vaccines arriving mid-pandemic? Stick with $13?

### Central decision
- **How should SII price Covishield** — hold the announced **$13/dose**, cut toward the **~$10 J&J / AstraZeneca not-for-profit benchmark** (or lower), or adopt a **differentiated / two-part structure** (pandemic "access" price now vs. "value-based" later; separate prices for the Indian government, the Indian private market, and GAVI/low-income exports) — so as to recover the $200M+ at-risk investment while managing political, ethical, capacity, reputational and competitive pressure and the uncertainty about efficacy, durability and new entrants?

### Key takeaways
- **Cost floor vs. investment recovery:** SII's *marginal* cost per dose is very low (routine vaccines sell at $0.18–$3 at >40% margin); $13 is about amortizing sunk/at-risk investment, not unit cost — so there is huge room to price down without losing money on the marginal dose. The real question is what return is appropriate on a pandemic vaccine and over what volume to spread the investment.
- **Differential / tiered pricing is the natural answer** (and SII already implies it): near-not-for-profit for **Indian government + GAVI + low/lower-middle-income exports** (access, ethics, the June commitment, India's <$10/capita health budget); a **higher Indian private-market price** (Exhibits 2A/2B show private prices run 2–4× public); **value-based** pricing for high-income for-profit sales later. This maximizes access and volume while recovering investment from those who can pay — SII's historical model and the industry norm (China public $0.10–6 vs. private up to $100).
- **Two-part / temporal pricing:** a low "broad access" price while COVID is an emergency, then transition to value-based/seasonal pricing once endemic and competition normalizes — and credibly commit to step-downs "as scale is realized," which also pre-empts backlash and differentiates SII from Pfizer/Moderna.
- **Competitive framing:** J&J $10 and AstraZeneca not-for-profit make $13 look like profiteering; Moderna/Pfizer set the upper anchor. SII should price **at or below the not-for-profit benchmark for government/GAVI channels** to protect its identity as the affordable, high-quality manufacturer — capacity and the Oxford licence give it volume, not pricing power.
- **Ethics and optics:** SII's brand ("your survival should not be determined by where you live") and Cyrus Poonawala's public boasts about undercutting Western pharma make a visibly high pandemic margin reputationally expensive — favour transparent cost-plus / modest-margin pricing for the pandemic phase with a stated glide path down.
- **Manage uncertainty with a staged commitment:** unknown efficacy/durability, possible dosing changes, and next-gen entrants argue for announcing an **access price plus a mechanism** (volume-linked step-downs, review points) rather than one fixed number, with the value-based phase contingent on how the disease and competition evolve.
- **Recommendation shape:** ~**$3–5/dose (at cost + minimal margin)** for the Indian government and GAVI/low-income channels; a modestly higher Indian private-market price (still far below Western levels); value-based pricing reserved for high-income for-profit sales in the endemic phase; a public commitment to reductions as scale builds — rather than defending a flat $13.
