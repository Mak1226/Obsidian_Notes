# Cottle-Taylor: Expanding the Oral Care Group in India (Brief Case) — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Cottle_Taylor_Expanding_the_Oral_Care_Group_in_India.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBS Brief Case (Quelch & Zalosh). October 2009. **Brinda Patel**, oral-care marketing director for **Cottle-Taylor's** India division, has a data-driven 2010 toothbrush plan projecting **+20% unit sales**. Her boss **Michael Lang** (VP Marketing, Greater Asia & Africa), under pressure to offset declining US revenue, calls it conservative and wants **25–30% unit growth**, arguing Cottle should (a) raise toothbrush ad/promo spend **above 12% of sales** and (b) stop splitting the ad budget **equally across three messages** — convert non-brushers / increase brushing frequency / trade consumers up to mid-range and premium brushes — as a similar push did in **Thailand in 2007** (+25% units vs. a 19% baseline after +3% ad spend). Patel has **two days** to produce a revised plan and a projected income statement for both her plan and Lang's, with stated assumptions — while doubting India (80% on <$2/day, 78% rural) will replicate Thailand, and worrying that a 20%-price-increase-with-no-volume-loss assumption and recession-era cost pressure make Lang's plan fragile.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Cottle:** $11.5B revenue (2009), 200+ countries, ~50% of revenue from emerging markets; 2004–09 sales +8%/yr, EPS +14%/yr; oral care anchors emerging-market growth. **Cottle India = 38% of the Indian oral-care market**; sells through **450,000+ outlets**.
- **India context:** pop. 1.16B; **~37% below the $1.25/day poverty line, ~80% on <$2/day**; **78% rural** (but 64% of total expenditure); rural buyers extremely price-sensitive, buy small sizes at tiny grocers; urban higher-income buyers use supermarkets and prefer large sizes.
- **Oral-care behaviour:** centuries of **Neem-twig** use; oral-care sector grew **10%** 2008→09. **>50% of rural India uses no toothbrush**; among brushers only **23% brush twice daily** (Figure 1); only **2% of Indians see a dentist regularly**; rural Indians **5x more likely** to reject modern products; an **IDA/Cottle study** found campaign-exposed rural consumers **twice as likely** to adopt brushing within a year.
- **The replacement gap:** **747.1M toothbrushes sold in India in 2009**, but only **8.6% of users replace within 3 months** (IDA recommends every 3 months) — 4 in 5 keep a brush until it breaks.
- **Cottle's toothbrush position:** basic toothpaste/toothpowder gross margin fell **31% → 25%** since 2004 (herbal/ayurvedic entrants) → Patel and Lang agreed to **lean on Cottle's toothbrush manufacturing advantage** (50+ years, no import reliance). 2009: toothbrushes = **17.5% of Cottle India oral-care sales** (toothpaste 48.7%, toothpowder 33.8%). **Cottle = 46% of Indian toothbrush units**; 2009 toothbrush sales **$70.1M**, gross margin **44%**; Patel's 2010 plan → **$100.2M** (+20% volume × +20% price). Competitors: Hinda-Daltan ~21%, SarIndia ~11%, cheap imports ~22%.
- **Product line:** Low-end manual (Complete, Sensitooth, FreshGum, Surround, Kidsie); Mid-range manual (Zagger, Directionflex); Battery (Swirl, Swirl Refills). Exhibit 7 manufacturer gross margins: low-end 47–50%, mid-range 40–52%, Swirl 40% / refills 60%, toothpaste 23–25%, toothpowder 21% — but per-unit *value* is far higher for mid-range/battery (Swirl $6.40 vs. Complete $0.15 manufacturer price).
- **Communications (2009):** 9% of gross toothbrush sales on advertising + 3% on promotion/merchandising (10% of that was off-invoice trade allowances); target age **20–35**, rural + urban; ad split **50% TV / 30% print / 15% outdoor / 5% radio**; weighted to mid-range to encourage trade-up.
- **Three messages** — Patel's predecessor split the budget **equally**; Lang wants that ended:
  1. **First-time brushing** (convert non-users)
  2. **Increase brushing / replacement frequency**
  3. **Trade up to mid-range or premium products**
  - Patel leans toward **(1) and (2)** (rural conversion + overall incidence); Lang is expected to favour **(3)** (premium unit sales, à la Thailand).
- **Distribution:** 20 large distributors (urban), ~250 medium (semi-urban), vast **"seed" distributors → 300,000+ village outlets**. **Patel saw no value in seed distributors** — each small outlet ≈ **$150 revenue/year**.
- **Planning pressure:** 2010 comp tied to regional performance; underperforming regions "reorganized." Lang wants an outline of each message's audience and short/long-term benefits, a recommended ad-dollar allocation with assumptions, and P&Ls for both plans; also asks what 2009 would have looked like with **Thailand's product mix**, and: **would a 3% ad increase necessarily raise revenue and profit?**

### Numbers to work
- 2009: sales $70.1M, GM 44% → gross profit ~$30.8M; ad+promo 12% = ~$8.4M.
- **Patel's plan:** 70.1 × 1.2 (vol) × 1.2 (price) ≈ **$100.9M** (case: $100.2M); gross profit ≈ $44M; ad+promo 12% ≈ $12M.
- **Lang's plan (illustrative):** 70.1 × 1.3 × 1.2 ≈ **$109.4M**; ad at 15% of sales ≈ $16.4M — about **$4M more** than Patel's. Gross profit at 44% ≈ $48M → incremental ≈ **$4M gross profit for ~$4M more ad spend** ⇒ the extra 3% **does not clearly pay for itself in-year** unless volume growth beats 30% or the mix shifts to higher-value brushes.

### Central decision
- In two days, **revise the 2010 India toothbrush plan toward Lang's 25–30% unit target**, deciding: (1) whether to raise ad/promo above 12% of sales and prove whether +3% ad spend actually lifts revenue and profit; (2) how to **reallocate the ad budget across the three messages** instead of an equal split; (3) what **unit-volume and revenue forecast** to commit to and defend — reconciling Lang's Thailand-based premium-led thesis with a mostly rural, sub-$2/day market, an untested 20% price increase, and cost pressure.

### Key takeaways
- **Forecast bottom-up from the addressable base:** the big *unit* prize is message (2) (replacement/frequency among 747M current brushers, only 8.6% replacing on time) and message (1) (converting non-brushers); the big *profit-per-unit* prize is message (3). The 20%→30% gap must be explained by explicit assumptions about which message drives which increment.
- **Message economics:** (1) rural non-users — slow, low near-term revenue, needs IDA co-marketing and low-price brushes, but builds the long-run base and pre-empts rivals; (2) existing brushers — fastest payback, minimal behaviour change; (3) urban trade-up via supermarkets/dentists — highest revenue and gross profit per unit, but India's income base is far below Thailand's.
- **Does +3% ad spend pay off? Not automatically:** ~$4M more spend needs ~$9M+ more sales at 44% GM just to break even in-year — justify it on demonstrated incremental response (Thailand's +6pp over baseline) and the long-term market-development value of message (1), and target it at segments where response is provable (likely 2 and 3), not blanket rural (1).
- **India ≠ Thailand:** a premium-led plan over-forecasts here. A defensible plan likely weights **(2) heaviest**, sustains **(1)** at ~2009 level (or via IDA cost-share) for the strategic base, and adds **(3)** spend only in urban/supermarket/dentist channels.
- **Price-increase caveat:** don't assume the 20% hike is volume-neutral for price-sensitive rural buyers — model a scenario with real elasticity, especially for entry brushes.
- **Reconsider seed distributors:** message (1)'s rural non-users are reached through exactly the 300,000+ village outlets Patel dismissed — revisit for low-price entry SKUs if rural conversion is a strategic priority.
- **Org dynamics:** Lang approves the plan and comp is now target-linked, so there's pressure to commit to 30% — but a rigorously-assumed 20–25% plan, with the incremental-spend scenario fully costed, is the stronger position.
