# Measuring CSR: A Menu of Options (Quick Case) — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Measuring_CSR_A_Menu_of_Options.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBI Quick Case (fictitious). **Patrick Stocks**, national marketing manager for the South African arm of **Deluxe Chicken** (market-leading multinational fried-chicken chain; 40 SA restaurants, 12,000 SA employees), runs the brand/purpose function including CSR. Deluxe already funds two ~USD $150,000/3-year programs (a student scholarship fund and children's food donations). A sports-marketing agency now pitches **Soccer4Good** — a South African youth-soccer NGO (last year 32,000 kids aged 6–12, 2,500 volunteer coaches, priority to communities with no school sports) that a competitor, Quick Pies, is dropping. An international consultant is helping Deluxe build **refined impact measurement tied to spend, especially marketing**. Patrick's manager, Stephenie Ndlovu, asks him to draft a proposal on whether to take Soccer4Good to the board — the "why," the time and money commitment, and **how to measure impact both for the children and for the business**.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Existing CSR:** scholarship fund + children's food donations, each ~USD $150,000 over 3 years. The food program donates *funds* to ECD centres/children's homes/schools — **not** Deluxe-branded product, because "we mainly make fried chicken… not the healthiest option." Brand benefit so far is soft: small local/regional press via partners' social media, partner praise for UN SDG commitment, some positive LinkedIn attention — but **no data linking it to the bottom line**.
- **Soccer4Good opportunity:** SA-based, growing; last year **32,000 children aged 6–12**, **2,500 volunteer coaches**; partners with schools/communities for playing space; **first priority to communities with no formal school sport**; reported gains in children's **communication, teamwork, accountability, resilience**. Currently measures impact only by *number of children enrolled*.
- **Commitment:** ~USD $150,000 over 3 years; **all logistics handled by the sports-marketing agency**; only a few days/year of Deluxe staff time plus light ongoing review of messaging, progress and metrics.
- **The task:** draft a board proposal — articulate the rationale and the time/financial investment, and design a framework to measure impact **on the kids** and **positive business outcomes for Deluxe**, feeding the international consultant's cross-country measurement work.

### Central decision
- **What should Patrick's proposal say, and how should results be tracked and measured?** Should Deluxe recommend the Soccer4Good sponsorship, on what rationale, and what measurement framework captures both the social impact on children and the business return — chosen from the "menu" of ways to measure CSR?

### Key takeaways
- **The measurement menu (inputs → outputs → outcomes → impact):** *inputs* (dollars, staff days); *outputs/activities* (children enrolled, coaches, sites, meals, press mentions); *outcomes* (life-skills gains, school attendance, nutrition); *long-term impact* (life trajectories). Each is progressively harder to attribute — be explicit about **contribution vs. attribution** and about what is realistic to collect for a low-cost, agency-run program.
- **Social-impact metrics for Soccer4Good:** go beyond enrolment — validated life-skills scales, school attendance/retention, coach and teacher surveys, share of participants from underserved communities, with a **baseline and a comparison group** so change can be shown (accept proxies where rigorous data isn't feasible).
- **Business-outcome metrics:** brand awareness/consideration/favorability lift, share of voice and earned-media value, strength of cause association, foot traffic/sales in communities near activities, employee engagement/volunteering, and fit with corporate + UN SDG reporting — while being honest about the limits of tying CSR to profit.
- **Strategic "why":** same audience (children/families), a real unmet need (sport where schools have none), turnkey (minimal staff time), and it lets Deluxe associate with health and activity — a useful counter to the "we sell fried chicken" tension that caps the food program's brand upside. **Risks:** inheriting a competitor's cast-off cause, "cause-washing" scepticism, and over-concentrating CSR on children's programs.
- **Governance:** set objectives and KPIs *before* committing; fix a review cadence (annual plan review + light monitoring); define reporting lines and how results feed both the board decision and the consultant's cross-country framework; choose metrics you can actually gather and that map to the SDGs and to marketing-spend accountability.
- **Don't measure only what's easy** (enrolment, press mentions) — that invites gaming and misses whether lives or the business's position actually changed; balance ease of collection against decision-usefulness.
