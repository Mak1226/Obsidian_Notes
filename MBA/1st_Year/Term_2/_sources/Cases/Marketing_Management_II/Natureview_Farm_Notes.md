# Natureview Farm — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Natureview_Farm.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBS Brief Case (Fleming). February 2000. **Christine Walker**, VP marketing of **Natureview Farm** — a Vermont maker of natural/organic-positioned refrigerated cup yogurt ($13M revenue in 1999, ~24% of the natural foods channel, prized 50-day shelf life) — must find a way to grow revenue **to $20M by the end of 2001** so the 1997 VC investor can exit at a good valuation (organic-food companies trade at **1.5–2.1× revenue**). The senior team is split three ways: (1) expand **6 SKUs of the 8-oz line into 2 supermarket regions**, (2) expand **4 SKUs of the 32-oz size nationally into supermarkets**, or (3) launch **2 children's multipack SKUs in the existing natural foods channel**. Two of the three require entering the supermarket channel — a major strategic break that risks the natural foods retailer relationships that built the brand. Walker must pick one and give the CEO "a coherent point of view and an action plan."

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Category:** supermarkets sell **97% of all yogurt**, natural foods stores 3%. Supermarket yogurt grew ~3%/yr; **natural foods grew ~20%/yr** (5-yr CAGR projected 15%; organic-yogurt units at supermarkets projected +20%/yr 2001–06). Organic food market $6.5B (1999) → $13.3B (2003). Discount rate **8%**. A new plant ($30M) is **not** needed for any option; capex roughly equal.
- **Product mix (1999):** 8-oz = 86% of revenue (12 flavors), 32-oz = 14% (4 flavors). Cases: 12 cups (8-oz), 6 (32-oz), 4 packages (multipack).
- **Channels & margins:**
  - **Supermarket (3-tier):** mfr → distributor (**15%** margin) → retailer (**27%**); **broker fee 4% of mfr sales**; **slotting ~$10,000 per SKU per chain** (year 1 only); quarterly trade promotion required (trade-promo ads $7,500–$15,000 per ad per retailer). Big rivals (Dannon, Yoplait) spend >$60M/yr on marketing.
  - **Natural foods (4-tier):** mfr → wholesaler (**7%**) → distributor (**9%**) → retailer (**35%**); no cash slotting, but **one free case per new SKU** in year 1; higher retail prices, lower price sensitivity.
- **Prices & costs (Exhibit 3):** retail — supermarket 8-oz **$0.74** / 32-oz **$2.70** / multipack **$2.85**; natural foods 8-oz **$0.88** / 32-oz **$3.19** / multipack **$3.35**. Natureview mfg cost: 8-oz **$0.31**, 32-oz **$0.99**, multipack **$1.15**. Stated gross margins: 8-oz **36.0%**, 32-oz **43.6%**, multipack **37.6%**.
- **Competition:** **Horizon Organic** (flush from IPO) is the top threat to supermarket entry; Dannon rumored to be launching organic "Bright Vista"; supermarket private-label organic under consideration — the window may be closing.

### The three options (Exhibit 6 incremental unit volume)
| | **Option 1** — 6 SKUs 8-oz, 2 supermarket regions (NE + West) | **Option 2** — 4 SKUs 32-oz, national supermarket (64 chains) | **Option 3** — 2 children's multipack SKUs, natural foods channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incremental units (yr 1) | **~35.0M** | **~5.5M** | **~1.8M** |
| Implied incremental revenue (mfr) | **~$16M** | **~$9M** | **~$3.3M** (≈10% of natural-foods category $) |
| Contribution per unit* | **~$0.13** | **~$0.62** | **~$0.69** |
| Gross contribution | **~$4.5M** | **~$3.4M** | **~$1.2M** |
| Marketing | **$1.2M per region** (TV/radio/outdoor/print) = **$2.4M** + quarterly trade promo | **$120k per region** (10% of the 8-oz plan) + trade promo **twice/yr** | **$250,000** |
| Slotting (one-time, yr 1) | 6 × $10k × ~20 chains ≈ **$1.2M** | 4 × $10k × 64 chains ≈ **$2.56M** | free cases ≈ **2.5% of mfr sales (~$83k)** |
| Incremental SG&A | **+$320,000** ($200k sales + $120k mktg) | **+$160,000** | **none** (within current capability) |
| Approx. net contribution yr 1 | ~break-even (big upside as slotting is one-time and category grows 20%) | modest after slotting, strong thereafter | **~$0.9M** (highest margin, lowest risk) |
| Reaches $20M goal? | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No** (~$3.3M of a ~$7M gap) |
| Channel conflict | **High** (hits competitors' core; natural-foods retailers see brand 15%+ cheaper) | **Moderate** (32-oz is a natural-foods "heavy user" size; less visible to big rivals) | **None** |

\* Derive manufacturer selling price by working retail price back through the channel margins, then contribution = MSP − COGS − broker fee / free-case cost.

### Central decision
- **Which growth strategy should Walker recommend** to hit **$20M revenue by end-2001** and maximize the revenue-multiple valuation for the VC exit — the high-volume, high-cost, channel-conflicting **8-oz supermarket entry**; the higher-margin, lower-profile, slotting-heavy **32-oz national supermarket entry**; or the clean, profitable, but sub-target **children's multipack in the natural foods channel**?

### Key takeaways
- **Model every option the same way:** MSP from retail-back-through-margins → contribution/unit → × incremental units → minus advertising, one-time slotting, trade promotion, and incremental SG&A. Result: Option 1 ≈ $16M revenue / ~$4.5M gross contribution but ~$4M+ of year-1 costs; Option 2 ≈ $9M / ~$3.4M / ~$2.6M one-time slotting; Option 3 ≈ $3.3M / ~$1.2M / ~$0.3M cost.
- **Only Options 1 and 2 reach $20M.** Option 3 is the most profitable per dollar and lowest-risk but **leaves the revenue goal unmet** — and the exit is valued on revenue.
- **Channel conflict is the core strategic risk:** natural-foods retailers (24% of Natureview's identity, higher-margin partners) will see the brand cheaper at supermarkets; the broker is already unhappy; de-listings and price concessions could cascade. Option 3 avoids it; Option 2 provokes the loyal base more than it looks (32-oz is *their* size) but draws less fire from Dannon/Yoplait; Option 1 attacks competitors' bread-and-butter.
- **Capability gap:** Natureview has won on guerrilla marketing + shelf life; supermarkets demand quarterly trade promotion, slotting, scanner-data management, dedicated sales staff, and budgets it cannot match. Option 1 bets the company on an unproven model; Option 2 needs 64-chain national distribution in 12 months; Option 3 is within current means.
- **Timing:** Horizon's IPO cash, a rumored Dannon organic launch, and supermarket private label mean the organic-yogurt supermarket window is closing — argues for entering **now** (1 or 2), not later.
- **A defensible recommendation:** **Option 2** best balances the goals — it reaches $20M, has the highest per-unit contribution, leverages the shelf-life advantage, attracts less competitive notice, familiarizes supermarket shoppers with the brand as a bridge to a later 8-oz push, and is cheap to support — accepting the big one-time slotting bill and the "won't recruit new users" limitation. Option 1 maximizes revenue/valuation and first-mover share *if* Natureview can fund and staff it; Option 3 only if the board will accept missing $20M. A phased play (3 now to fund and protect the base, then 2 as the supermarket entry) is also reasonable.
