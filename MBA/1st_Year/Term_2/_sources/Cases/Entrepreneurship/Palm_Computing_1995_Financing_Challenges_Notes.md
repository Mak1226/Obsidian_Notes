# Palm Computing, Inc. 1995: Financing Challenges — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Palm_Computing_1995_Financing_Challenges.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBS case. August 1995. **Donna Dubinsky** (President/CEO) and **Jeff Hawkins** (founder, chairman, CTO, major shareholder) have spent ~15 months raising money to finish and launch **Touchdown** — a $299, shirt-pocket "connected organizer" that syncs to a PC (this becomes the PalmPilot). The PDA category is toxic after Apple's Newton and Palm's own **Zoomer** flopped (~10,000 units sold), so venture money is scarce and cheap. Palm has **$1.5M in the bank** (~$250k/month burn) and four options: a flat ~$30M round with strategic partner **Alpha Computers** (+VC), a **down round** from ABC Venture Capital ($1M at $9M — near liquidation value), keep courting other partners, or an **outright acquisition by U.S. Robotics** (~$50M in USR stock, Palm run as an independent division). USR has 30 days of exclusivity.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **The pivot:** after partners (Casio, GeoWorks, Tandy) under-supported the Zoomer and blocked Zoomer II, Palm decided by mid-1994 to build the **whole product** — hardware + OS + apps + desktop software — itself, breaking the "industry wisdom" that software firms depend on hardware firms. Investor Bruce Dunlevie: "hang out and bleed to death, or bet the company on a riskier strategy."
- **Touchdown economics:** specs and mock-up done Aug 1994, planned ship Jan 1996; COGS ~$100/unit ⇒ low inventory and working-capital risk. Palm can *finish development* but **cannot fund inventory build or a marketing launch**; needs ~$5M (ideally $1–2M from a big-name corporate partner, VCs for the rest).
- **Funding history:** 1991 Merrill Pickard + Sutter Hill ($500k each) + Tandy ($300k, 10% cap); Sept 1992 round 2 (~$3M, Newtek Ventures $1.5M); Jan 1994 round 3 — Credit Lyonnais $3M at a **$30M valuation** (CL tried to renege; Dubinsky held them to it).
- **Failed partner talks:** Radio Shack (soured by Zoomer); **Megacommunications International** (wanted full IP ownership including OS source code — Palm refused to cede OS control); **Alpha Computers** (term sheet ~complete Feb 1995, then 4 months of stalling; demanded a simultaneous-launch clause and other protections — "protecting Alpha instead of maximizing the opportunity").
- **The four current options (case, "Making a Financing Decision"):**
  1. **Alpha Computers** — $1M at ~$30M (flat round); VCs likely add ~$2M; some unfavorable contract terms, but Alpha turned more flexible once a rival suitor appeared.
  2. **ABC Venture Capital** — $1M at **$9M** (down round ≈ liquidation value); hope existing VCs match; re-price later on Touchdown traction.
  3. **Other partners** (Europe/Asia, less cynical about PDAs) — push them to decide.
  4. **U.S. Robotics acquisition** — ~$50M in USR stock (USR ~$75/share); Palm operates as an independent division; Dubinsky joins USR exec staff; USR funds Palm "as much as needed"; champion **Jon Zakin** (SVP Bus. Dev.) is a true believer.
- **Constraints:** $1.5M cash (~6 months runway); 30-day USR exclusivity/no-shop; Dubinsky won't decide without Hawkins. Hawkins: "the USR deal would be like getting married after the first date."

### Central decision
- **Which financing path should Palm take** to get Touchdown to market — a flat round with a strategic (Alpha), a dilutive VC down round (ABC), continued partner search, or selling the company outright to USR — weighing runway, dilution/valuation, distribution and manufacturing access, control/independence, and founder upside?

### Key takeaways
- **Financing in a dead category:** post-Newton/Zoomer sentiment makes a defensible pre-money almost impossible; ABC's $9M mark is essentially the company's liquidation value — the price of raising while the narrative is against you.
- **Strategic vs. financial investor:** USR offers distribution (retail + modem channel), deep pockets, manufacturing, and conviction — but costs the independence Palm had just fought to win; the whole strategy pivot was about *not* being hostage to partners who don't share the vision.
- **Don't pursue partners serially:** "you can't row with one oar" — Palm repeatedly bet everything on one negotiation (Megacomm, then Alpha) and lost months each time.
- **Big-company deal teams optimize for protecting the parent,** not the opportunity — watch for creeping protections and outright control/IP grabs.
- **Valuation as option:** raise a little now at a low mark and re-price on early product results, vs. locking in an acquirer before the product proves itself.
- **(Beyond the case)** USR did acquire Palm in Sept 1995; the PalmPilot launched in 1996 and became a landmark success.
