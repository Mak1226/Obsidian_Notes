# Tata's Air India: Brand Repositioning and Revitalization Challenges — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Tatas_Air_India_Brand_Repositioning_and_Revitalization.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Ivey case (Nitin Gupta). January 27, 2023 — one year after **Tata Sons** formally took back control of **Air India** (which Tata founded in 1932 and lost to nationalization in 1953). Decades of government mismanagement left the airline with **₹839 billion of accumulated losses (FY2021)**, a reputation for delays, cancellations, broken seats and indifferent service, dated systems and old aircraft. CEO **Campbell Wilson** (from Singapore Airlines, appointed June 2022) has set out to make Air India the **"carrier of choice for passengers worldwide"** — capturing **30% of India's domestic market in five years** and building global hubs to compete with Emirates and Singapore Airlines — backed by a ₹150 billion turnaround plan, a **470-aircraft order (~$100B)**, a Vistara merger, and London brand consultancy **Futurebrands**. The class question: what is the right path to reposition and revitalize the Air India brand and make it a leading global airline?

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **The acquisition:** Oct 2021 the government sold Air India + Air India Express to **Talace** (a Tata subsidiary) for **₹180 billion**; control transferred Jan 27, 2022. Tata now owns four airlines — **Air India** and **Vistara** (full-service; Vistara a Tata–Singapore Airlines JV) plus **Air India Express** and **AirAsia India** (low-cost). Tata keeps the **Maharaja** mascot.
- **Wilson's three-phase plan:** **taxi** (fix what tarnished the brand — delays, meals, website, seats), **take-off** (accelerated investment in systems, people, equipment, aircraft), **climb** (after 5 years, "very good → world-class"). Chandrasekaran's four focus areas: best-in-class customer service, most technologically advanced airline, modern fleet, in-flight and off-flight hospitality.
- **Market:** India projected to be the world's fastest-growing air-traffic market by 2040; the government worries about international traffic lost to Emirates (Dubai) and Qatar Airways (Doha). Domestic share (Exhibit 1): **IndiGo ~56% (289 aircraft)**; Air India fell to **8.7% — its lowest ever**; then Vistara, AirAsia India, SpiceJet, Go First, Akasa.
- **Tata-ecosystem alliances (Exhibit 2):** TajSATS (improved meals), Indian Hotels (crew accommodation), Tata Technologies (3D-printed obsolete seat parts, fleet/maintenance digitization), Tata Business Excellence (process improvement), Air India SATS (ground handling), TCS ("One Tata Operating Network," Tata Neu super-app), Jaguar Land Rover (cabin design/branding).
- **HR turnaround:** ~13,000 staff (8,000 permanent), **average age 53** (retire at 58); ~4,500 took VRS, ~4,000 retiring by 2025; adding 1,200 ground staff, 4,200 cabin-crew trainees, 900 pilots (2023); senior hires from TCS, Tata Digital, MakeMyTrip, Singapore Airlines/Jet Airways (Exhibit 3); town halls, recognition programs, **~3% employee stock plan**.
- **Technology:** **$200M** digital investment over 5 years (website, app, IFE, notifications; GPT-4 for FAQ/UI); Salesforce CRM; SAP ERP; Meta Workplace for internal comms.
- **Combining airlines:** merge **Air India + Vistara** (Tata 74.9%, SIA 25.1%; SIA invests ₹20.59B; close ~March 2024; **Vistara brand to be phased out**); combined domestic share ~26% (vs. IndiGo 57%). Merge **AirAsia India + Air India Express** (Tata bought AirAsia's 16%).
- **Fleet:** narrow-body 70 (54 in service), wide-body 43 (33 operational); **$400M refurbishment** of the entire fleet (new seats, cushions, screens; JPA Design + Trendworks) — **delayed by supply chain to mid-2024**; leasing 36 aircraft; increasing UK (34→48/week) and US (34→40/week) frequencies (Russia-airspace avoidance helps Air India); deploying capacity into more profitable **international long-haul** and domestic **corporate routes**.
- **Historic order (Feb 2023):** **470 planes** — Airbus 250 (A320/A321 Neo + 40 A350), Boeing 220 (190 737 Max + 20 787-9 + 10 777), option for 370 more — ~$100B list price; enables non-stop India ↔ every major world city; Tata's credit rating → cheaper financing; Power-by-Hour engine deals with GE and Rolls-Royce. Deepening **Lufthansa** codeshare; in talks to join **Star Alliance**.
- **Year-1 improvements:** positive earnings **FY2023**; revenue per mile 4.5 (FY20) → ~6.5; highest-ever passenger revenue (₹31B, Dec 2022); yield up via agent incentives + new revenue-management software; **beat IndiGo on on-time performance** (Jul–Sep 2022) for the first time. But cost per available seat mile stayed high.
- **Unresolved issues:** consistent service excellence is the hard part in a "moribund, indifferent" PSU culture; old aircraft/tech still fail; **overstaffed but can't rightsize** (employment terms frozen until March 2023); **strong union opposition** to the new service agreement and to the new-captain hiring plan (pilots fear "artificial distinction between old and new pilots"); integrating younger Vistara/AirAsia cohorts with an older Air India workforce; dependence on government-owned **AIASL ground handling** (delays, understaffing, mishandled baggage); **Nov 2022 "pee-gate"** (₹3M penalty, captain suspended) — reputational damage.

### Central decision
- **How should Wilson reposition and revitalize the Air India brand** into a "carrier of choice worldwide"? Specifically: what should the brand stand for (how much Maharaja heritage / "new India" narrative to keep vs. reinvent); how to **sequence the rebrand against operational fixes** so the promise isn't broken on delivery; how to **architect four merging airlines** into a coherent portfolio; how to shift a PSU service culture through internal branding against union resistance; and what milestones define the turnaround?

### Key takeaways
- **The gap is delivery, not the logo:** Air India connotes delays, broken seats, "pee-gate," indifferent PSU service; the target is a premium, reliable, world-class full-service carrier representing "the new India" and a global hub connector. Closing that gap is 90% consistent execution.
- **Substance before symbolism — sequencing:** the three phases (taxi → take-off → climb) encode it — fix punctuality (done vs. IndiGo), meals (TajSATS), seats/IFE ($400M, delayed), digital ($200M), and ground handling (AIASL) first; go loud on a rebrand only as the experience becomes reliably good. A premium promise on a cannibalized-fleet reality invites backlash.
- **Brand architecture → two brands:** consolidate to **Air India** (full-service, global, premium — absorbing Vistara's service standards, crew and product, and its SIA/Star Alliance relationship) and **Air India Express** (low-cost, short-haul — absorbing AirAsia India), with the **Maharaja** as the shared heritage device. Manage loyalty-programme migration and the fact that Vistara currently out-ranks Air India on perceived quality — migrating that equity in is the point.
- **Internal branding is the crux and the hardest part:** a 53-average-age, unionised, PSU-legacy workforce with frozen terms and unions fighting the service agreement and pilot plan. Levers: senior external hires, VRS + retirements, 5,000+ new frontline recruits with real service training, town halls, recognition, a 3% stock plan — but the rebrand only sticks with a negotiated union settlement, clear service standards, and accountability.
- **A global reason to exist:** use the 470-plane order and India's geography to make Air India the **hub carrier** connecting SE Asia / the subcontinent to Europe and North America non-stop, recapturing traffic from Gulf carriers; deploy new wide-bodies with competitive business/premium-economy for high-yield traffic; deepen Lufthansa codeshare and join Star Alliance.
- **Leverage the Tata name and ecosystem** (Taj food, Indian Hotels hospitality DNA, TCS/Tata Digital, JLR/JPA design, Tata Business Excellence, cheap aircraft financing) as both a differentiator and a trust transfer the old Air India lacked.
- **Metrics:** on-time performance and completion rate; NPS / complaint volume; load factor and yield / RASM; progress toward 30% domestic share and international/hub volume; brand tracking (trust, consideration, premium perception) pre/post rebrand; key-talent attrition and employee engagement; and the financial glide from daily losses to sustained profit.
- **Risks:** promising world-class before delivering it; union conflict stalling the service overhaul; integration drag from four carriers and two cultures; supply-chain delays outrunning customer patience; and residual PSU service incidents undoing brand-building.
