# Mahindra Financial Services: Restructuring for Growth — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Mahindra_Financial_Services_Restructuring_for_Growth.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> IIM Bangalore case (Sourav Mukherji). **Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services (MMFSL)** — a rural/semi-urban NBFC (part of the Mahindra Group), ~1,300 branches, 5M+ customers, ~20,000 employees, INR 7,206 crore income (FY18) — financed tractors and then, from 2002, non-Mahindra vehicles too. Its **geography/branch structure** (generalist branch teams doing both lending and collection, connected up through territory→region→zone→country-group managers, with HQ "Product Heads" who had responsibility but **no authority** over branches) buckled under growth: branch managers spread too thin, product plans unimplemented, an "either-or" tug between sales and collection letting receivables age just as the RBI tightened the NPA clock from 150 to 90 days. In **January 2016** MMFSL restructured. The case describes why, how, and the results; the class question is which structure was right and how the transition and its downsides were handled.

## Case Notes

### Key facts — why restructure
- **Relational lending model:** rural customers have no documented credit history; the branch manager and team extend first formal credit, customize repayment to crop cycles, give EMI holidays for calamities. Cash collections (often in person). Branch = generalist, a **profit center**, sized by local business volume.
- **Old structure:** field ops = Territory → Region → Zone (grew to 9) → new **Country Group Manager** layer → VP Operations; plus an HQ **product overlay** (Product Heads for each vehicle type + collection) who set strategy and pushed it via **policy directives with no reporting line** to branches — "a support function to them."
- **Growth strains:**
  - Branch teams optimized *overall* targets, not product mix or collection quality; "the DNA continued to be about M&M products"; non-M&M and even M&M dealers felt neglected; "the same customer expects different treatment buying a car vs. a tractor."
  - Branch managers **spread too thin** — recovery-strong ones over-focused on collection and lost sales; auto-strong ones pushed auto and neglected other segments.
  - **"Either-or" collection:** executives did the easy collections; hard ones "moved forward"; more attention to old debt than to newer, lower-age receivables — fatal because profit depends on **arresting the forward flow**, and RBI was cutting the **NPA definition 150 → 90 days**.
  - Too many layers → Product Heads **bypassed the hierarchy** to reach branch managers directly and acted as informal "mentors" → branch managers took orders from **multiple bosses** → accountability confusion.
  - By end-2015: stagnating segment market shares, deteriorating collection quality, falling employee productivity.

### Key facts — the restructuring
- **Process (three stages, ~1 year):** a team under VP Operations Rajnish Agarwal — (1) diagnose causes, (2) design and evaluate options, (3) implement — with employee involvement throughout. A ~100-employee survey confirmed the overload/role-overlap/accountability-ambiguity hypotheses; benchmarking showed most comparable banks used **product** structures.
- **The choice:** keep geography, overlay product on geography (matrix/hybrid), or a **pure product structure**? Geography gave cohesive, motivated, profit-center branches; a product split risked destroying that cohesion. MMFSL chose a **pure product structure** as the best enabler of its strategic objectives (customer centricity, deeper OEM relations, role clarity).
- **New structure (Exhibit 4):** split at the top into a **Business vertical and a Collection vertical**; the advisory Product Head role dismantled. Business Heads of 7 divisions (Auto, Farm Equipment, Commercial Vehicles, Light Motor Vehicles, Refinancing, Direct Marketing) report to the Operations Head; a **National Collection Head** runs the separate collection vertical. Verticals run **down to the field** — the branch stays a physical site but is **logically split into 7 units**, each with its own chain of command; branches go from **profit centers → service-delivery centers**; no single Branch Manager, instead multiple product **Area Managers**; the senior-most manager handles admin.
- **Collection model (built in-house, no benchmark):** classify customers by defaults — **soft (0–1), hard (2–3), NPA (4+)**; caseloads sized by a month of field visits — ~**250 / 150 / 80–100 contracts per executive per month**; collection stays with the **business team for the first 12 months** of a contract, then moves to the collection team.
- **Managing the change:** heavy, repeated communication — MD **Ramesh Iyer** "talked about it everywhere," formally and informally, because "if people have apprehensions they suffer in silence and it shows up in their work"; a **role-efficacy study** matched people to sales vs. recovery roles and honored preferences; framed as "**replacing breadth with depth → more scope for growth**"; **conclaves/forums** to negotiate authorities and boundaries and get team buy-in; explicit patience ("if it doesn't improve within a year we admit failure and pull the plug") and no reverting at first trouble.
- **Support functions realigned:** HR restructured (with Aon Hewitt) to mirror the verticals — **Centers of Excellence** design policy, **HR Business Partners** (matrixed: solid line to HR Ops, dotted to Business Heads) customize by vertical, **Service Delivery** handles low-customization field transactions; "one size fits all" → bespoke incentives/recruitment/holidays per asset class. L&D dissolved as a standalone unit. Finance and Legal to follow.
- **Results:** performance improved over the following two years — collection efficiency up, every vertical grew (including passenger cars, "not MMFSL's forte") — with **no attrition** and higher engagement, avoiding the usual post-restructuring dip. Product Heads became "**mini-CEOs**"; "each vertical is almost equal to what entire MMFSL was five years ago."
- **Acknowledged downsides:** (1) loss of the cohesive branch entity → silo risk (countered by "we are one company" messaging and cross-vertical contact); (2) poor fit for **low-opportunity rural markets** where a generalist can cover multiple functions (piloting a low-cost trainee format); (3) visible vertical "stars" are **easier for competitors to poach**.

### Central decision
- **What structure should MMFSL adopt** — keep geography, overlay product on geography (matrix/hybrid), or move to a pure product structure with a separate collection vertical — to fix branch-manager overload, unimplemented product plans, the sales-vs-collection "either-or," and the aging of receivables under tighter NPA norms? And **how should the transition be designed** and the resulting downsides (lost branch cohesion, thin-market fit, key-person poaching) be managed?

### Key takeaways
- **Structure follows strategy and scale:** the generalist geography model fit a small, tractor-centric business; product/customer diversification plus regulatory pressure made product accountability, role clarity, and collection discipline the priorities — which a "responsibility without authority" product overlay could never deliver (it bred multi-boss confusion).
- **Geography vs. product vs. hybrid:** geography = cohesion and profit-center identity but buried product accountability; matrix = cohesion but persistent dual-reporting ambiguity; **pure product** = clear KRAs and front-line product targets, dedicated non-M&M attention (removing group-conflict concerns), and "mini-CEO" ownership — at the cost of the branch's identity.
- **Separate collection from sales** to protect newer receivables ("arrest the forward flow"); with no benchmark, build your own model (default buckets, field-researched caseloads, business-team collection for the first year).
- **Change management that worked:** a deliberate year-long diagnose→design→implement process; relentless leader communication; competency/preference matching via a role-efficacy study; a growth framing; negotiated role clarity in forums; and visible patience with a pre-committed review point.
- **Align support functions to the new design:** HR (then Finance, Legal) restructured to the verticals — CoE + matrixed Business Partners + Service Delivery — moving from standardized to bespoke policies.
- **Manage the trade-offs, don't deny them:** reinforce one-company identity against silos; keep a generalist low-cost format for thin markets; accept greater poaching exposure as the price of visible, capable talent.
- **Governance shift:** verticals run like independent companies, eventually judged purely on P&L, with the corporate center reduced to capital allocation and ROI.
