# Wilkerson Co. — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Wilkerson_Co.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBS case (Kaplan; a re-set of "Destin Brass"). March 2000. **Wilkerson** makes three products for water-purification equipment makers: **valves** (its original precision product, stable 35% gross margins), **pumps** (a commodity where competitor price cuts have crushed reported margin to ~19.5% and pre-tax profit to 3%), and **flow controllers** (customized, more components and labor, many small runs — where a recent 10% price rise met "no apparent effect on demand"). Wilkerson allocates all manufacturing overhead at **300% of production-run direct-labor cost**, and controller Peggy Knight's task force has just gathered the data to build an **activity-based cost system**. The question: why are overall margins collapsing when the current system says pumps are the only weak line, and what should Wilkerson do about pricing, mix and operations?

## Case Notes

### Key facts (March 2000)
- **Operating results (Exhibit 1):** Sales $2,152,500; DL $271,250; DM $458,000; **MOH $806,000** — machine-related $336,000, setup labor $40,000, receiving & production control $180,000, engineering $100,000, packaging & shipping $150,000. Gross margin $617,250 (29%); SG&A $559,650; **pre-tax income $57,600 (3%)**.
- **Current costing:** overhead = **300% of production-run DL cost** (DL is $25/hr incl. fringe). Reported margins (Exhibit 2): valves 34.9%, **pumps 19.5%**, **flow controllers 41.0%**.
- **Product data (Exhibit 3):** valves — 4 components, DM $16, DL 0.40 hr, 0.5 machine hr; pumps — 5 components, DM $20, DL 0.50 hr, 0.5 machine hr; flow controllers — 10 components, DM $22, DL 0.40 hr, 0.3 machine hr.
- **Monthly volumes/drivers (Exhibit 4):**

  | | Valves | Pumps | Flow Ctrl | Total |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Units | 7,500 | 12,500 | 4,000 | 24,000 |
  | Machine hours | 3,750 | 6,250 | 1,200 | 11,200 |
  | Production runs | 10 | 50 | 100 | 160 |
  | Shipments | 10 | 70 | 220 | 300 |
  | Engineering hours | 250 | 375 | 625 | 1,250 |

- **Overhead study findings:** machine costs ∝ machine hours; a **setup per production run**; receiving & production control work is **per run** (regardless of run length or component value); packaging & shipping work is **per shipment**. At peak demand the plant ran 12,000 machine hrs / 180 runs / 400 shipments with no delays or overtime → there is **unused capacity**.

### ABC cost-driver rates
- Machine-related: $336,000 / 11,200 mh = **$30 / machine hour**
- Setup labor: $40,000 / 160 runs = **$250 / run**
- Receiving & production control: $180,000 / 160 runs = **$1,125 / run**
- Engineering: $100,000 / 1,250 hr = **$80 / engineering hour**
- Packaging & shipping: $150,000 / 300 shipments = **$500 / shipment**

### ABC results (per unit)

| | Valves | Pumps | Flow Controllers |
|---|---|---|---|
| DM | $16.00 | $20.00 | $22.00 |
| DL | $10.00 | $12.50 | $10.00 |
| **ABC overhead** | **$20.17** | **$25.70** | **$83.38** |
| **ABC unit cost** | **$46.17** | **$58.20** | **$115.38** |
| Actual price | $86.00 | $87.00 | $105.00 |
| **ABC gross margin** | **~46%** | **~33%** | **≈ −10%** |
| (vs. current system) | 34.9% | 19.5% | 41.0% |

- Flow-controller overhead breakdown per unit: machine $9.00, setup $6.25, receiving/prod-control **$28.13**, engineering **$12.50**, packaging/shipping **$27.50**.

### Central decision
- **Why are Wilkerson's margins collapsing when the current system flags only pumps?** Build the ABC system from the overhead study and Exhibit 4 drivers, recompute product costs and margins, and decide what to do about **pricing, product mix and operations** — especially for flow controllers.

### Key takeaways
- **The 300%-of-DL system is badly wrong:** it over-costs **pumps** (high volume; only 50 runs, 70 shipments, few engineering hours) and massively under-costs **flow controllers** (low volume; 100 runs, 220 shipments, half of all engineering hours, 10 components). Flow controllers *look* like the star at 41% but are actually **sold below cost**; pumps *look* like the problem at 19.5% but are near the 35% target once costed properly.
- **The 10% flow-controller price rise with no demand loss** was the tell — the product was severely underpriced.
- **Pricing:** raise flow-controller prices substantially toward ABC cost + target margin; hold pump prices (they're ~33% GM, so matching competitor cuts is defensible); reset target prices product-by-product on ABC costs; protect/grow the quietly profitable valve line.
- **Operations (what ABC points to):** setup and receiving/production-control cost is **per run**, not per unit — flow controllers run ~40 units/run vs. pumps ~250 and valves ~750, so consolidate into **fewer, larger runs**; packaging/shipping is **per shipment** — cut 220 flow-controller shipments via consolidation and order minimums; **reduce component count / standardize** flow controllers; question their engineering intensity (625 of 1,250 hours); charge customers for small orders and frequent deliveries.
- **Capacity caveat:** rates here are on *actual* March volume, so they load the cost of unused capacity onto products; a practical-capacity (time-driven ABC) denominator would lower rates slightly and isolate cost-of-unused-capacity — but the direction of the mispricing is unchanged.
- **Also worth a look:** SG&A is 26% of sales and not in product cost — a customer-profitability analysis (small customers drive many shipments) is a logical next step.
