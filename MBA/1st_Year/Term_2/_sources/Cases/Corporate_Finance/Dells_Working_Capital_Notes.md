# Dell's Working Capital — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Dells_Working_Capital.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBS case (Richard Ruback). After FY1996 (ended 31 Jan 1996) — revenue **$5.3B, up 52%** vs. industry ~31%, net income **$272M (5.1% of sales)** — Michael Dell expects to keep growing double digits and needs a plan to **finance future growth**. Dell's **direct, build-to-order** model gives it almost no finished-goods inventory (DSI ~32 days vs. Compaq ~70+), a short **cash conversion cycle (~40 days, sometimes negative on an operating basis)**, and modest capex. Students test whether working-capital efficiency plus retained profit can self-fund ~50% growth in FY1997 — and model the funding gap if Dell instead carried competitor-level inventory.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **FY1996 P&L (Exhibit 4, $M):** Sales 5,296; COGS 4,229; gross margin 1,067; operating expense 690; operating income 377; **net income 272** (~5.1% margin). FY95 net 149; FY94 net **(36)**; FY93 net 102.
- **FY1996 balance sheet (Exhibit 5, $M):** Cash 55; **short-term investments 591**; AR 726; inventory 429; total current assets 1,957; total assets 2,148. AP 466; accrued 473; total current liabilities 939; **long-term debt only 113**; equity 973.
- **Working-capital ratios (Exhibit 2), Q4 FY96:** DSI 31, DSO 42, DPO 33 → **CCC ≈ 40 days** (down from ~57 in Q1 FY94). DSI fell from 55 (1993) to ~32 (1995) — Table A shows rivals at ~48–73.
- **Why inventory matters:** components ≈ 80% of a PC's cost and component prices fall ~30%/yr, so low inventory avoids obsolescence losses and lets Dell adopt new tech fast (Pentium share FY94 1% → FY96 75%; shipped Win95 on launch day; avoided the flawed-Pentium recall cost that hit Compaq). New tech reaches market in ~35 days, ~⅓ of competitors' time.
- **Cautionary history:** 1990–93 detour into indirect retail (CompUSA, Staples, Price Club); Aug 1993 **first loss** ($76M Q2, incl. $71M inventory/notebook write-offs); margin fell to 2% vs. 5% target; only $32M cash. Michael Dell: "watching the speedometer… running out of gas." Exited indirect retail July 1994; set ROIC and CCC targets in late 1995.
- **Outlook:** analysts expect PC market +20%/yr for 3 years; Dell expects to outgrow the industry again; FY1996 constrained somewhat by component shortages.

### Central decision
- **Can Dell finance projected growth (~50% in FY1997) internally**, from operating cash flow and its working-capital model — or does it need external financing? How much external funding would be required (a) at FY1996 working-capital efficiency and (b) if Dell had carried 1993-era or competitor-level inventory?

### Key takeaways
- **The direct model is a financing machine:** near-zero inventory (DSI ~31) + collecting from customers (DSO ~42) while stretching suppliers (DPO ~33) gives a very short CCC, so each extra dollar of sales needs only a small amount of net working capital.
- **Rough FY1997 math:** operating assets net of operating liabilities are only ~10–11% of sales, so 50% growth (~+$2.6B sales) needs only ~$250–300M more operating investment. At a ~5% net margin (no dividends), retained earnings (~$400M) alone cover it — and Dell also holds **$591M in liquid short-term investments** as a further cushion. **Conclusion: no external financing needed; Dell can self-fund the growth.**
- **Counterfactual:** if Dell held inventory at competitor levels (say DSI ~70–80), incremental working-capital needs roughly double and the internally-generated funds no longer cover growth — external financing would be required. This isolates the value of the low-inventory model.
- **Sustainable growth rate** is very high here precisely because operating-asset intensity is low and margins/asset turns are strong; growth is limited more by operations (component supply, execution) than by finance.
- **Broader lesson:** manage the balance sheet and cash flow, not just the P&L — the 1993 stumble was a cash/inventory failure, not a demand failure.
