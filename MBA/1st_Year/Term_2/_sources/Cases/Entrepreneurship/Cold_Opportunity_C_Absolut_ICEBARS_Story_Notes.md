# Cold Opportunity (C): The Absolut ICEBARS Story — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Cold_Opportunity_C_Absolut_ICEBARS_Story.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Part C. "If your stakeholder won't take your calls, get the stakeholder to call you." Bergqvist builds an ICEBAR with a doorway cut in the shape of an **Absolut bottle** and real Absolut bottles on ice shelves, then (via the Swedish Tourist Board) blasts a press release — "ICEHOTEL ICEBAR, −5°C, Jukkasjärvi" — to thousands of outlets. Absolut's New York PR agency sees the coverage, Absolut's marketing manager phones him ("Are we involved in this?"), and a deal follows: "**buy-in is better than sell-in**." From there the concept scales — a doubtful Absolut lets him test **ICEBAR Stockholm (2002)**, it becomes the best-selling Absolut outlet in the city, and Absolut returns asking to "proceed," leading to a **franchise** model (Milan, London, Tokyo, Copenhagen…), an ice-export business, and eventually a Virgin Galactic tie-up.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Getting the "no-reply" partner:** the Absolut-bottle doorway + bottles on the bar + a mass press release → Absolut's own NY agency raises it internally → Absolut calls Bergqvist. He admits Absolut isn't yet involved "but that is our wish"; Absolut sends someone and they sign.
- **Terms Bergqvist protected:** ads had to name **Jukkasjärvi**; local partnerships (the village church's Ice Chapel, regional hotels) mattered as much to him as the Absolut logo.
- **ICEBAR Stockholm, 2002:** Absolut called it the wrong location, "too risky," told him not to use the trademark inside and to bear the risk himself. After 6 months it was Stockholm's top Absolut seller; after a year Absolut came back to "proceed" → a **franchise agreement**; rollout to Milan and other cities.
- **The model:** vodka served *in* ice (ice glasses), not on ice; cover charge up to ~€15 for 15 minutes; slogan "Purer than water"; 600–1,000 clients per bar per night; ~6 weeks to build a bar; sculptures refreshed every 6 months by visiting artists.
- **Arctic Export:** Torne River ice shipped worldwide in containers (~4/day to Tokyo, ~1 krona/glass to ship); ~500,000 ice glasses a year; ice became one of Swedish Lapland's most successful exports.
- **Cross-promotion flywheel:** every ICEBAR press mention also mentions the ICEHOTEL → drives tourism back to Jukkasjärvi. By 2006, 135+ artists competed for 30 ICEHOTEL slots.
- **Other partners:** Fritz-Hansen, SAAB, Philips, SJ rail, SAS; 2008 Gävle Energi (CO₂-negative goal by 2015); 2009 Virgin Galactic (first trip planned 2012).

### Central decision
- **How should Bergqvist extend an experiential brand internationally** — licensing/franchising vs. owning locations, how much control to keep over experience quality, and how to manage deep dependence on a single anchor partner (Absolut) while the concept scales?

### Key takeaways
- **Crazy-quilt / committed partners drive growth:** the venture expands by adding stakeholders who opt in and co-invest, each pre-committing resources and shaping the direction.
- **"Pull" beats "push":** manufacturing visible proof (a bar that looks like an Absolut ad, a best-selling test site) converts a reluctant partner far better than pitching.
- **Brand equity transferred both ways:** ICEHOTEL credibility gave urban ICEBARs instant meaning; ICEBAR publicity feeds the ICEHOTEL — the experience, not the ice, is the asset that travels.
- **Franchising fits an experience concept** once it is codified (build spec, artist rotation, ice supply chain), but the quintessential experience still depends on Torne River ice and local partnerships — the quality-control tension.
- **Keep pushing the frontier of "doable":** start with what is doable and worth doing at low cost, then extend it partner by partner (rafting → workshop → hotel → bar → global franchise → space).
