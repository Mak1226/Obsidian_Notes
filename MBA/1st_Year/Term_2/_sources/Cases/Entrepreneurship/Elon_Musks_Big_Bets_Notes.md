# Elon Musk's Big Bets — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Elon_Musks_Big_Bets.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> HBS case (Yoffie, Baldwin, Kaufmann). April 2017. Tesla just posted a record 25,000-car quarter and its market value has passed Ford and GM to make it the most valuable US automaker — despite never turning a profit (~$2.7B cumulative losses) and burning ~$2.5B a year combined with SolarCity. Musk is simultaneously running four moon shots: **scale Tesla 10x + deliver autonomy**, build the **$5B Gigafactory** (105 GWh/yr, more than all 2015 li-ion output), turn Tesla into an "end-to-end clean energy" company via the **SolarCity acquisition**, and take humanity to Mars with **SpaceX** — while also launching The Boring Company and Neuralink. The ventures are cross-financed with public capital, convertible debt, strategic investors, loans between the companies, and Musk's own credibility and pledged stock. The class question: is this a sustainable, replicable model of entrepreneurship, or "a slick businessman dragging foolish investors along on grand cash-burning bets"?

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Track record:** Zip2 → sold to Compaq $300M (1999); X.com/PayPal → sold to eBay $1.5B (2002). Then reinvested nearly everything into SpaceX, Tesla and SolarCity.
- **SpaceX (2002):** Musk put in ~$100M; first 3 launches failed; on the brink of collapse Sept 2008 ("more than his fortune at stake — his credibility") before the 4th succeeded. Milestones: ISS docking (2012), rocket landing (2015), **re-flew a used Falcon 9 (March 2017)** — reuse could cut launch cost ~30% ($61M → $43M). Pad explosion Sept 2016; 2015 revenue −6%, ~$260M operating loss; ~$12B valuation (2015). Competitor: Bezos's Blue Origin.
- **Tesla:** founded 2003 (not by Musk); Musk joined 2004 as chairman/funder, became CEO 2008 (after ~$55M of his own money), IPO 2010. **"Secret plan" (2006):** sports car → fund an affordable car → fund an even cheaper one. Roadster (2008) → **Model S** (2012, $70–100k; sales <5k → 150k by 2016; CR 99/100 then recommendation revoked on reliability; battery fires + NHTSA probe) → **Model X** (2015; falcon-wing-door and seat-latch quality issues) → **Model 3** ($35k, prototype March 2016, ~375,000 pre-orders in a month). **Autopilot** "public beta" → first fatality May 2016 (NHTSA ruled driver at fault, Jan 2017). Musk's target: **500,000 cars/year by 2018** — off ~84,000 made in 2016, and a history of missed dates.
- **Tesla financials (Exhibit 3a, $M):** revenue 204 (2011) → 2,014 (2013) → 7,000 (2016); net loss every year (~$773M in 2016; ~$2.7B cumulative); ~$4,000 lost per vehicle in 2015; total debt $8.6B (2016, incl. SolarCity); >$9.3B raised to date (Exhibit 2a). Two-dozen-plus senior managers left in 2016 ("long hours, mission creep, tense culture"; Musk a self-described "nano-manager").
- **Gigafactory:** "ore in one side, battery packs out the other" — extreme vertical integration against industry outsourcing/dealer norms (Tesla also sells direct + built 800+ superchargers). ~$5B ($2B Tesla, funded via a $2B convertible-debt raise; Panasonic $1.5–2B, makes the cells). Scope kept expanding: EV cells → 1/3 for stationary storage → Model 3 motors/gearboxes; capacity tripled 35 → **105 GWh** by 2018; plans for a 3rd–5th Gigafactory; "Gigafactory 2" = SolarCity's Buffalo plant.
- **Battery / energy context (Exhibits 4–5):** li-ion market $21B (2014) → ~$76B (2020), demand shifting from consumer electronics (60%→24%) to energy storage (7%→38%) and autos (18%→30%); pack cost ~$350/kWh in 2016 (was >$1,000 in 2007; Tesla maybe $150–200); DOE parity target $125/kWh by 2022; Panasonic 41% of cell market. **Tesla Energy:** Powerwall (residential, ~$3,000) + Powerpack (utility); >100,000 reservations worth ~$1B by mid-2015 ("might not" convert).
- **SolarCity:** founded 2006 by Musk's cousins (Lyndon & Peter Rive); Musk chairman, largest shareholder, main backer; no-money-down solar leases/PPAs; installed base 300,000; revenue $60M (2011) → $538M (2016) but debt grew faster (~$5B long-term); cumulative operating losses ~$980M (2014–15); spending 42% above revenue; **continuous need for financing.**
- **The acquisition:** June 2016 all-stock offer (~$2.6–2.8B); **Tesla stock fell 10.5%** the next day. Musk: renewable generation was always in the 2006 master plan; ~$150M claimed synergies. Skeptics (JP Morgan, WSJ, Forbes): little brand/customer/channel/tech overlap; a debt-laden **bailout** of family-connected founders. Governance: most directors of both boards recused; only **2 independent SolarCity directors** approved. Revised offer Aug 2016 ~$25.83/share (10x peers' revenue multiple, but far below the $86 all-time high); >85% of Tesla shareholders approved Nov 2016.
- **Financing squeeze (conclusion):** Musk + companies owe ~$6B (incl. ~$500M held by Musk via personal credit lines); companies lend to each other (SpaceX bought $165M of SolarCity bonds). Musk stakes: Tesla/SolarCity 21%, SpaceX 54%; net worth ~$13B but little cash. Despite promises of no new capital, Tesla raised ~$1.4B (March 2017) and **Tencent bought 5% for $1.8B**; Musk added $25M of his own.

### Central decision
- **Is Musk's model — running several simultaneous moon shots, cross-financed among unprofitable companies and propped by his credibility and stock — sustainable and replicable, or a house of cards?** Concretely: (1) Was the SolarCity acquisition a strategic masterstroke or a conflicted bailout that endangers Tesla? (2) What should Tesla's focus be — mass-market carmaker, battery/energy company, or integrated clean-energy platform? (3) What, if anything, is transferable to an ordinary entrepreneur, and where do first-principles thinking, extreme risk tolerance, portfolio cross-financing and a punishing leadership style break down?

### Key takeaways
- **First-principles vs. analogy:** rejecting "industry wisdom" produced real breakthroughs (reusable rockets ~30% cheaper; battery cost down an order of magnitude; vertical integration + direct sales) — but also over-optimistic targets (500k cars by 2018; Mars by 2025) and genuine safety/quality/regulatory costs (Autopilot "beta," battery fires, recalls).
- **Betting the company, repeatedly:** SpaceX (2008) and Tesla (2013) each came one event from failure and were saved by a single success plus outside capital — this is survivorship, not a reliable method.
- **Capital dependence:** the strategy needs continuous access to cheap capital and a high stock price; ~$6B group debt, three unprofitable firms, ~$2.5B combined burn, and inter-company loans mean a downturn or a confidence shock could unwind it.
- **Do the ventures reinforce each other?** Plausible: EV ↔ Gigafactory batteries ↔ stationary storage ↔ SolarCity solar = an end-to-end clean-energy system. Skeptics: minimal brand/customer/channel/tech overlap; SolarCity looked like a debt rescue with conflicted governance; SpaceX shares only Musk's attention and some engineering talent.
- **Leadership cost:** record output and "most valuable US automaker" vs. senior-manager exodus, related-party deals, and insider-heavy boards.
- **Transferable:** question inherited assumptions; sequence an expensive vision through a "secret plan"; vertically integrate where it yields a real cost/capability edge; raise ahead of need. **Not safely transferable:** betting the company each round, financing one venture with another, relying on a personal-credibility premium, and running multiple companies at once.
