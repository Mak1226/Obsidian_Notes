# Blaine Kitchenware, Inc.: Capital Structure — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Blaine_Kitchenware_Capital_Structure.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> April 2007. **Victor Dubinski**, CEO and founder's great-grandson of **Blaine Kitchenware (BKI)** — a debt-free, cash-rich (family owns 62%) maker of branded small kitchen appliances — is prompted by a banker's remark that BKI is "over-liquid and under-levered" and that "your shareholders are paying a price for that." A private-equity group has sniffed around an LBO; the banker notes BKI could capture the same value itself by borrowing to buy back stock. The class task is the classic trade-off-theory exercise: quantify how a **leveraged recapitalization** (borrow + spend excess cash to repurchase shares) changes EPS, ROE, WACC, firm value, credit metrics, family control, and acquisition/dividend flexibility — and recommend whether the board should do it.

## Case Notes

### Key facts (FY2006, $ thousands unless noted)
- **Revenue $342,251; Net income $53,630; EBIT $63,946; EBITDA $73,860** (EBITDA margin ~21.6%, among the strongest of the peer set in Exhibit 3). Organic growth weak; nearly all top-line growth came from acquisitions.
- **Balance sheet:** zero debt; **cash & marketable securities $230.9M** (down from $286M two years earlier); book equity $488.4M; total assets $592.3M. Strongest balance sheet in the industry; revolver cancelled in 2002.
- **Returns lag:** **ROE 11.0% vs. peer mean 25.9% / median 19.5%**. Shareholder return ~11%/yr (2004–06) vs. peer ~16%. **EPS fell $1.29 (2004) → $1.07 → $0.91 (2006)** on ~59.05M average shares, hurt by dilutive stock-funded acquisitions.
- **Dividends:** $28.3M in 2006; **payout ratio 52.9%** (up from 35.0% in 2004) — management and family consider this trend unsustainable.
- **Ownership:** founders' descendants + trusts hold **62%**; family heavily represented on the board; family has no interest in selling and wants to keep acquiring (esp. weak beverage-appliance segment, 2% share).
- **Culture:** extremely debt-averse — borrowed beyond seasonal needs only twice ever (WWII, 1970s oil shock), repaid ASAP.
- **Market data:** stock ~$16.25, near all-time high; equity beta 0.56 (Exhibit 3). Exhibit 4: Treasuries ~4.9–5.0%; Moody's Aaa 5.88% / A 6.35% / Baa 6.72%. Effective tax rate ~30.8% in 2006, **expected to rise to the 40% statutory rate**. 2007 revenue growth guided at ~3% assuming no acquisitions.

### Central decision
- **Should BKI undertake a leveraged recap** — e.g. use ~$209M of the cash hoard plus ~$50M of new long-term debt to repurchase roughly 14M shares at a modest premium (~$18.50) — and, more broadly, what is the right ongoing capital structure, dividend policy and cash level? Should the board approve it given family control and acquisition ambitions?

### Key takeaways
- **Why the status quo destroys value:** all-equity + a large idle cash pile means (1) forgone **interest tax shield**, (2) **cash drag** depressing ROE and ROIC, (3) capital that dilutive acquisitions have been eroding. A firm this stable and asset-light can comfortably carry some debt.
- **Recap math to run:** pro forma EPS and ROE both rise (fewer shares, tax-shield benefit); WACC falls modestly; firm value increases by roughly the PV of the debt tax shield (≈ tax rate × debt); interest coverage stays very high, so an investment-grade rating (A/Baa) is easily maintained — distress risk is minimal.
- **Governance angle:** if the family doesn't tender, its ownership % **rises** (reversing the post-IPO dilution) and the board gets more room on dividends per share — reasons some family members would welcome it.
- **Costs / objections:** loss of the acquisition "war chest" and financial flexibility, exposure to a cyclical end-market (appliance sales track housing/GDP), and a genuine break with a conservative family culture.
- **Typical recommendation:** do a **one-time, moderate** leveraged repurchase (not an aggressive LBO-style capital structure) — enough to capture the tax shield and lift returns while keeping ample liquidity for acquisitions; pair it with a sustainable dividend policy.
