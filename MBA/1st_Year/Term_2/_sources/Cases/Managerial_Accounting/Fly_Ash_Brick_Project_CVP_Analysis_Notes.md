# Fly Ash Brick Project: Feasibility Study Using CVP Analysis — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Fly_Ash_Brick_Project_CVP_Analysis.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Ivey case (Mitra & Hajela). April 2013. **Rajiv Sharma**, an experienced construction-sector man, wants to build a **fly-ash brick plant** (his "dream project"); friend **Alok Gupta** is interested but doubtful and they agree to have a consultant CVP-test the plan. Plant capacity is **4 million bricks/year**; the partners expect to sell **2.4 million/year at Rs 7,000 per 1,000 bricks (Rs 7/brick)**. Investment is **Rs 10 million** (Rs 8M fixed assets + Rs 2M working capital), funded Rs 6M by the partners and **Rs 4M by a bank loan at 12%**; Sharma manages the plant for a Rs 50,000/month salary; 5-year life, negligible salvage, working capital recovered in full. The task: compute variable cost per brick, annual fixed costs, contribution margin, break-even volume, and profit and margin of safety at 2.4M bricks — then give a go / no-go verdict with sensitivity to price and capacity utilization.

## Case Notes

### Cost structure
- **Variable cost per brick (Exhibit 4, per month for 200,000 bricks = 2.4M/yr):** fly ash 250,000 + gypsum 250,000 + lime 300,000 + sand 40,000 + electricity 10,000 + labour 50,000 = **Rs 900,000/month ÷ 200,000 = Rs 4.50/brick**. (Lime is the largest single input, ~33% of variable cost.)
- **Annual fixed costs:**
  - Routine expenses (Exhibit 3: rent 50k + admin 10k + office 5k + lighting 10k + misc 20k = 95k/mo) → **1,140,000**
  - Personnel (Exhibit 5: workers 100k + office assistant 20k + watchman 15k + drivers 25k = 160k/mo) → **1,920,000**
  - Sharma's salary (opportunity cost — they'd pay a replacement Rs 50k/mo) → **600,000**
  - Interest on loan (Rs 4,000,000 × 12%) → **480,000**
  - Depreciation (Rs 8,000,000 ÷ 5, no salvage) → **1,600,000**
  - **Total fixed cost ≈ Rs 5,740,000/year** (Rs 4,140,000 excluding non-cash depreciation)
- **Contribution margin per brick = Rs 7.00 − Rs 4.50 = Rs 2.50** (CM ratio ≈ 35.7%).

### CVP results
- **Accounting break-even** = 5,740,000 / 2.50 = **~2,296,000 bricks/year ≈ 57% of capacity** (≈ Rs 16.07M revenue).
- **Cash break-even** (exclude depreciation) = 4,140,000 / 2.50 = **~1,656,000 bricks/year**.
- **At the planned 2,400,000 bricks (60% capacity):**
  - Pre-tax accounting profit = 2,400,000 × 2.50 − 5,740,000 = **Rs 260,000/year** (razor-thin).
  - Operating cash flow ≈ profit + depreciation ≈ **Rs 1,860,000/year**.
  - **Margin of safety ≈ (2,400,000 − 2,296,000) / 2,400,000 ≈ 4.3%.**
- **At full capacity 4,000,000 bricks:** profit = 4,000,000 × 2.50 − 5,740,000 = **Rs 4,260,000/year** — strong.
- **Rough NPV (total project, 12%):** 5 × Rs 1,860,000 (annuity factor 3.605 ≈ Rs 6.71M) + Rs 2.0M working-capital recovery discounted (≈ Rs 1.14M) ≈ **Rs 7.85M PV vs. Rs 10M outlay → NPV ≈ −Rs 2.2M**. Simple payback on Rs 10M ≈ **5.4 years > the 5-year life**. (All sensitive to tax treatment and loan amortization, which the case omits.)

### Sensitivity
- **Price:** at Rs 6.50/brick, CM = Rs 2.00 → break-even ≈ 2,870,000 bricks > planned 2.4M → **loss**. A small price cut or input-cost rise (esp. lime) wipes out the thin margin.
- **Utilization is everything:** the project only clears accounting break-even at ~57% capacity and only becomes genuinely attractive well above the planned 2.4M, toward full capacity.
- **Financing gap:** the Rs 4M loan principal has no repayment source in the plan — salvage is negligible and the Rs 2M working-capital recovery only half-covers it.

### Central decision
- **Is the fly-ash brick plant financially feasible — go or no-go?** Compute variable cost/brick, annual fixed cost, CM, break-even volume and % capacity, profit and margin of safety at 2.4M bricks, and test sensitivity to price and utilization; then advise, weighing thin financials against policy/environmental tailwinds.

### Key takeaways
- **The plan is only marginally viable at planned volume:** ~Rs 260k profit and ~4% margin of safety at 2.4M bricks; break-even needs ~57% capacity utilization.
- **Very fragile to price and input costs:** a ~7% price decline (Rs 7.00 → Rs 6.50) makes it loss-making; lime and gypsum cost volatility is a real risk given the Rs 2.50 CM.
- **Feasibility hinges on demand near capacity:** at 4M bricks profit is ~Rs 4.3M — so the real question is whether the market genuinely supports volumes far above the conservative 2.4M estimate.
- **Structural weaknesses:** 5-year life with negligible salvage, payback longer than project life, negative NPV at a 12% hurdle on the base case, and no loan-principal repayment plan.
- **Judgement calls to flag in the analysis:** whether to load Sharma's salary (yes — opportunity cost), interest (financing vs. operating), and depreciation (exclude for cash/payback, include for accounting break-even) into fixed costs — each changes the break-even point.
- **Non-financial factors that support a "conditional go":** secure long-run fly-ash supply, government mandates/concessions for fly-ash bricks, a large housing/brick shortage, and environmental benefit (no topsoil mining, waste reuse, no thermal energy) — but also mason/buyer acceptance risk, entrenched clay bricks, new entrants, and IS 12894:1990 quality compliance. Recommend proceeding only with a credible plan to run well above 2.4M bricks and a fix for the loan-repayment gap.
