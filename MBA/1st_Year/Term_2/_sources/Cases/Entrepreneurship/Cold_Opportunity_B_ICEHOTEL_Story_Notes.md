# Cold Opportunity (B): The ICEHOTEL Story — Notes

📄 Case PDF: [[Cold_Opportunity_B_ICEHOTEL_Story.pdf]]

## Quick Read
> Part B. After the 1989 rain disaster, Bergqvist and the artists reframe the loss — "we are trying to preserve something that belongs to nature; let it be destroyed and make something new" — and the **spirit of ICEHOTEL** is born: treat cold, dark and impermanence as assets. A 1990 igloo art exhibition (Jannot Derit's 60 m² "Arctic Hall") leads, by accident, to guests asking to *sleep* in the igloo; over three years the igloos grow into a hotel that melts every spring and is rebuilt every winter. By 1993 ICEHOTEL exists, and Bergqvist wants **Absolut Vodka** as a partner — but after a Johnnie Walker rejection and four unreturned calls to Absolut's marketing head, he is stuck deciding how to land the partnership and how to build a recurring business around a product with a four-month life.

## Case Notes

### Key facts
- **Reframe of the contingency:** the melt is redefined as the point of the product — a hotel "on loan from the mighty Torne River" that returns to the rapids each spring.
- **Accidental product discovery:** 1990 igloo exhibition (Arctic Hall) draws visitors; one night foreign guests ask to sleep inside with reindeer hides and sleeping bags → the "hotel" use is found, not planned. Bigger, more elaborate igloos over the next 3 years.
- **The name:** coined on the spot to a journalist — "It's like… a hotel… an ice hotel."
- **The experience/offer:** sleep in sleeping bags on ice beds on reindeer skins, woken with hot lingonberry juice; constant −5°C inside vs. −25°C outside; Ice Chapel weddings; Absolut cocktails in ice glasses ("a drink *in* your ice, not ice in your drink").
- **Partner search by network:** the liquor-partner idea came from an ice artist whose father ran Johnnie Walker (Diageo); Johnnie Walker passed. Bergqvist wants **Absolut** for aesthetic/cultural/national fit (Swedish), but Absolut (Curt Nycander, head of marketing) won't return calls.
- **The pitch ingredients** Bergqvist keeps rehearsing: the Arctic night, pristine Torne River ice, international artists, adventurous guests, sleeping in an igloo, weddings, the annual rebuild, and Absolut cocktails in ice.

### Central decision
- **What does Bergqvist do next to get Absolut on board** when the company won't engage — and how does he turn a seasonal, melting attraction into a real venture with recurring revenue and a transferable concept?

### Key takeaways
- **Lemonade principle in full:** constraints (melting, remoteness, −25°C, darkness) *are* the value proposition; a "planned" entrepreneur would have tried to engineer them away.
- **Effectual growth = leverage contingencies + co-create with partners:** the hotel emerged from an art show and a guest's odd request; the business model is being assembled with whichever committed stakeholders show up.
- **Pull, don't push (sets up C):** rather than keep selling Absolut, Bergqvist will make something Absolut *wants to join* — "buy-in is better than sell-in."
- **Brand built with almost no ad budget:** press coverage, a striking name, and a genuinely novel experience do the marketing.
- **The core asset is an experience, not a building** — which is exactly what makes it replicable elsewhere (the ICEBARs in C).
